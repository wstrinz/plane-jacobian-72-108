# PROOF_PATTERNS — are the kill certificates instances of a template, or 20 one-offs?

**Date:** 2026-07-24. **Status: MINED + FULLY EXPANSION-VERIFIED (20/20), NEW
FILES ONLY.** New files: `proof_pattern_miner.py` (independent miner+verifier,
exact rational arithmetic, no floats), this doc. READ-ONLY on
`kill_certificates/` and on every existing script; nothing modified, nothing
committed.

**Headline.** Yes — and more sharply than the hypothesis asked. **All 20
certified certificates are instances of ONE template**, the *Rabinowitsch
telescope normal form*, verified by exact expansion on every one of them
(20/20, no exceptions). But the compression is smaller than that sounds,
because the template's *content* — a power-membership witness `sum A_i g_i =
M^k` — is genuinely different in each case. The real, quantified compression is
elsewhere and is three-fold:

1. **20 stored certificates are 12 distinct payloads** — 8 files are
   byte-identical re-derivations of a payload already on disk.
2. **12 distinct payloads are covered by 9 identities** — one certificate
   provably certifies another certificate's *different* generator system,
   including one **cross-category** case (`d2_threshold` ⟶ `harvest`).
3. **Each surviving identity covers an interval of `deg d2`, not one state** —
   and the interval extends to states never computed. Two brand-new states
   (`sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd23_dsig5`, `sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd22_dsig3`) were closed
   *predictively*, by rebuilding their generator systems from scratch and
   expanding a stored cofactor vector against them. Both give exactly `1`.

**And the negative that matters most:** the template closes **0 of the 29
NOT-YET-CERTIFICATED files**. Not one. The lane "mine the certificates to close
the open queue" is closed by this run.

## 1. The template: Rabinowitsch telescope normal form

Every certified system contains exactly one generator of the shape
`g_R = w*M - 1` (the saturation/Rabinowitsch generator; `M` is the product of
the recipe's `saturation_factors`, `w` the auxiliary variable). Write
`t = w*M`.

> **Template T1.** Let `A_i` be `w`-free and `k >= 1` with
>
> ```
>     sum_{i != R}  A_i * g_i   =   M^k                       (CORE)
> ```
>
> Then setting `C_i = w^k * A_i` and `C_R = -(1 + t + t^2 + ... + t^{k-1})`
> gives `sum_i C_i g_i = w^k M^k - (t^k - 1) = 1`.
>
> Conversely every certificate is of this form: substituting `w -> 1/M` kills
> `g_R`, so `A_i := M^k * C_i|_{w -> 1/M} = sum_j c_{i,j} M^{k-j}` (where
> `C_i = sum_j w^j c_{i,j}`) is a polynomial and satisfies (CORE), with
> `k = max_{i != R} deg_w C_i`.

The converse direction is not asserted on faith: the miner *extracts*
`(k, M, A_i)` from each stored certificate, checks (CORE) by exact expansion,
then *rebuilds* the normal-form certificate from `(k, M, A_i)` and checks that
it expands to `1`. Both checks pass 20/20.

**What this means operationally:** the `w`-machinery in every certificate is
boilerplate. The mathematical content of a kill is exactly *"`M^k` lies in the
ideal generated by the non-saturation generators, and here is the witness"*.
The `w` variable, the Rabinowitsch generator, and the telescoping geometric sum
carry no information and can be regenerated from `(k, M)` in one line.

Two sub-classes are worth naming because they are *strictly weaker than a
Gröbner computation*:

* **LINEAR core** (`harvest__a8_dd2-inf_dd10_dsig5`): all `A_i` are rational
  *scalars*. The kill is the statement that a `Q`-linear combination of 12 of
  the 16 master coefficients equals `gamma^8`. That is **linear algebra over
  `Q`**, not ideal membership — Singular's `lift` was never needed.
* **MONOMIAL core** (`harvest__sub2T2_a8_b0000_*`): all `A_i` are single
  monomials (here all equal to a scalar times `g3`), so after dividing out the
  common `g3` this too is a scalar linear relation, `sum lambda_i g_i =
  g3^2*gamma^3`. Also linear algebra.

2 of the 12 distinct payloads (and 3 of the 20 files) are therefore
**linear-algebra kills wearing Gröbner clothing**. The remaining 10 have
genuinely polynomial multipliers.

## 3. The family-level identities, and the held-out verification

### 3.1 Transfer

Define: certificate `P` **transfers** to system `Q` if every generator of `P`
that carries a *nonzero* cofactor occurs verbatim (name-keyed, exact monomial
equality) among `Q`'s generators. Then `P`'s cofactor vector, extended by zero,
is a candidate certificate for `Q` — which the miner then re-expands in `Q`'s
ring.

```
transfers found                          : 31
  all 31 expand to exactly 1
transfers onto NOT-YET-CERTIFICATED files: 0  (of 29 open files)
```

The transfer relation collapses the 20 certified files to a **greedy minimal
cover of 9 identities**:

| identity | covers |
|---|---|
| `phase_f2_sub2__...gz-s6` | s0, s1, s5, s6 |
| `phase_f2_sub2__...gz-s93` | s13, s18, s93 |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd22_dsig5` | dd20, dd21, dd22 |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a7_b1000_dd22_dsig3` | dd20, dd21, dd22 |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd21_dsig3` | dd20, dd21 |
| **`d2_threshold__a12_b1110_T2_d5`** | **`d2_threshold a12_b1110_T2_d5` + `harvest a12_b1110_T2_sup12`** |
| `phase_f2_sub2__...sz1_dz0_gz-s3` | itself |
| `phase_f2_sub2__...gz-s33` | itself |
| `harvest__a8_dd2-inf_dd10_dsig5` | itself |

The bolded row is the **cross-category** case: the `d2_threshold` certificate
(10 generators, 3676 cofactor terms) certifies the `harvest` system (14
generators) outright. The `harvest` run recomputed a *larger* certificate (4031
terms, 12 nonzero cofactors vs 9) for a system that already contained a
certified subsystem. That is a duplicated expensive Singular `lift`, in a
different category, for the same branch `a12_b1110_T2`.

Totals: all 20 files are 10085 cofactor terms / 9.8 MB on disk; the 9-identity
cover is 4689 terms / 4.5 MB; the 9 cores (`w`-boilerplate stripped) are 2741
terms. **10085 → 2741 terms, a 3.7x end-to-end compression with nothing lost.**

### 3.2 The `deg d2` window, and a held-out prediction that *verified*

The most interesting single row is `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd21_dsig5`. Its
active generator set is `{g0, g1, g2, g3, g8}` — only **5 of 10** generators
carry a nonzero cofactor, and every one of them is *independent of `deg d2`*.
The five generators that do depend on `deg d2` all get cofactor `0`.

So the identity is not about one state at all. It certifies every member of the
family for which those five generators are unchanged. To test that as a
*prediction* rather than a post-hoc description, `proof_pattern_miner.py
--predict` rebuilds a fresh System-4 generator set through
`kill_certificate_tools.material_sys4` — the same builder the producer uses,
never touched by the certificate — and expands the stored cofactor vector
against it:

| stored certificate | freshly built target system | result |
|---|---|---|
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd21_dsig5` | `sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd23_dsig5` | **CERTIFIES — `sum c_i g_i == 1`** |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd21_dsig5` | `sub2T2_a8_b1000_dd24_dsig5` | inapplicable: active generator `g3` changes |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd20_dsig3` | `sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd22_dsig3` | **CERTIFIES — `sum c_i g_i == 1`** |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd20_dsig3` | `sub2T2_a8_b0000_dd23_dsig3` | inapplicable: active generator `g7` changes |
| `harvest__sub2T2_a7_b1000_dd20_dsig3` | `sub2T2_a7_b1000_dd23_dsig3` | inapplicable: active generator `g7` changes |

`dd23` for the `a8_b1000_dsig5` branch and `dd22` for the `a8_b0000_dsig3`
branch are **states that had never been certified**, closed here at the cost of
one polynomial expansion (~25 s to rebuild the system, milliseconds to expand)
instead of a Singular `lift`. Neither was used in constructing the certificate.
That is the exact-expansion held-out verification the mining task asked for.

The window is **bounded, and the bound is sharp and mechanical**: the family
extends exactly until the first *active* generator starts depending on the
parameter. `dd24` and `dd23` respectively fail that test, and the miner reports
`INAPPLICABLE` rather than guessing. **Empirical law:**

> a certificate covers the whole interval of `deg d2` over which its active
> generator set is constant, and not one step further.

## 4. Interpolation: what was attempted and what came back

Clusters with ≥3 members: C1 (4), C2 (3), C3 (3). In **all three**, the
distinct-payload count is **1** — every member carries the *byte-identical*
cofactor vector. So the "interpolation as a rational function of the state
parameters" is the **constant function**, fitted trivially and exactly:

| cluster | parameters that vary across members | fit |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | `deg_d2`, `deg_sigma` (and the `y`-degrees of `d2`, `sigma`) | CONSTANT |
| C2 | `dd` ∈ {20,21,22} (and `ydeg_d2`) | CONSTANT |
| C3 | `deg_d1`, `deg_d2` (and `ydeg_d1`, `ydeg_sigma`) | CONSTANT |

**No cluster contains three distinct payloads**, so a genuine non-constant
interpolation was never possible on this data — the miner says so
(`"cluster has < 3 members"` on the distinct-only pass) rather than fitting
noise. The nearest thing to a real family with varying payloads is
`sub2T2_a8_b1000_dsig5` at `dd ∈ {20,21,22}`, which has 3 members but only 2
distinct payloads, and those two **disagree on monomial support** (dd20 uses 7
active generators with 476 stored terms; dd21/dd22 use 4 with 421) — so no
common support exists to interpolate over. The miner reports
`"members disagree on monomial support"`.

The honest summary of step 4/5 of the mining brief: **the coefficients do not
interpolate, because there is nothing to interpolate between.** What replaced
it — and what is strictly stronger — is §3.2: instead of fitting coefficients
as a function of `dd`, we *proved they are independent of `dd`* over an
explicit window, and verified that on two systems built from scratch.

## 6. Honesty section — what this does NOT show

* **Not a family theorem over the state space.** T1 is a *normal form*, not a
  parameterized proof. Knowing every certificate is a telescope tells you
  nothing about which states are killable. The witness `A_i` for (CORE) is
  different in each of the 12 payloads and is exactly the hard part; the
  template strips the easy part.
* **Zero open cases closed from the existing files.** 0 of 29
  NOT-YET-CERTIFICATED files receives a transfer. The two states closed in
  §3.2 were **not** on the open list — they are new states in an already-killed
  branch. The mining lane therefore **does not** help the 220 flag-cases, and
  the "133 state kills closed ZERO of 220 flag-cases" problem is untouched by
  anything in this document. If the flag-cases were the reason to run this,
  the answer is negative and the lane should close.
* **The transfer test is deliberately narrow.** Generators are matched by exact
  name-keyed monomial equality. A transfer that needs a variable renaming, a
  scalar rescaling beyond the producer's primitive normalization, or a
  generator that is a *combination* of the target's generators, will be missed.
  A negative here is "no transfer under exact matching", not "no transfer".
  I did not implement the harder search and do not claim its result.
* **The 9-identity cover is greedy**, not proved minimal. Given the block
  structure (the cover groups are disjoint except for the C9⟶C10 edge) it is
  almost certainly optimal, but I did not run an exact set cover.
* **`--predict` only supports System-4 (`sub2T2_*`) names**, because that is
  the builder with a clean re-entrant constructor. The `phase_f2_sub2` and
  `a12_b1110_T2` families were *not* probed predictively; their duplication
  (§2) is observed on stored files only.
* **The `deg d2` window law is empirical.** It was verified at 5 points (2
  positive, 3 negative). I did not prove from the builder source that the
  active generators are `deg d2`-independent in general; the miner checks it
  per-instance and refuses to extrapolate.
* **The degree law `deg C_R = (k-1)(1+deg M)` is exact by construction** (it is
  the degree of the telescope), but the *total* certificate degree carries an
  additional residual (0 to +7 here) that is not predicted.
* **20 is a small sample**, and it is not a random one — it is the set of kills
  that happened to be cheap enough to certify. The 29 failures are precisely
  the interesting ones and contribute nothing to this analysis. Any statement
  here about "certificates in general" is a statement about easy certificates.

## 7. Actionable items this run produced

1. **Stop re-deriving.** Before spending a `lift`, run the transfer test
   against the existing certificates — it is milliseconds. It would have saved
   8 of 20 Singular runs outright, plus the cross-category `harvest a12`
   duplicate.
2. **Trim dead generators before `lift`.** 21 of 161 generator slots end up
   with cofactor `0`; two certificates need only half their system.
3. **Record `(k, M)` in the certificate schema** and store the `w`-free core
   instead of the expanded cofactors (3.7x smaller across the corpus, and the
   full certificate is one line of reconstruction away).
4. **Order the queue by `(k-1)(1+deg M)`**, not by `deg_sigma`.
5. **The `deg d2` sweeps are over-sampled.** Each certified `dd` value is
