## 1. Introduction

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$. The plane Jacobian conjecture asserts that if $P,Q \in K[x,y]$ have $[P,Q] := P_xQ_y - P_yQ_x \in K^\times$, then $K[P,Q] = K[x,y]$. A counterexample can be normalised so that $[P,Q] = x^2$ after a change of variables, and Guccione–Guccione–Horruitiner–Valqui [GGHV22] classified all degree pairs $(\deg P, \deg Q)$ with $\max < 125$ for a hypothetical counterexample, discarding every one of them **except** $(72,108)$ and its transpose. Their Theorem 2.1 reads: a counterexample has $\max\{\deg P,\deg Q\} \ge 125$, or $(\deg P,\deg Q) \in \{(72,108),(108,72)\}$. They state explicitly (line 252) that discharging $(72,108)$ *"would increase the lower bound from 108 up to 125"*, and (line 268) that they could not solve the corresponding system of equations, *"thus it is left open"*.

Their Proposition 4.3, case $(8,28)$, reduces $(72,108)$ to a Laurent-polynomial problem at a single Newton corner, in two configurations. This paper proves that both configurations are empty.

> **Theorem A.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$. There is no pair $(P,Q) \in K[x,x^{-1},y]^2$ with $[P,Q] = x^2$ and
> $$N(P) = \mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(8,14),(8,16)\},\quad N(Q) = \mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),(2,1),(12,21),(12,24)\}$$
> (configuration (2)), nor one with the same corner sets together with $(0,8) \in N(P)$ and $(0,12) \in N(Q)$ (configuration (1)).
>
> **Corollary B.** Conditional on GGHV22 Proposition 4.3 being exhaustive (§1.2(i)) and on `[QQ1]` (Proposition 2.1, §1.2(ii)), any counterexample to the plane Jacobian conjecture has $\max\{\deg P,\deg Q\} \ge 125$.

The proof of Theorem A runs on remarkably little: **four polynomials, one polynomial identity, two slice families, one valuation cascade, and nine integers.** It factors through one intermediate object, an *admissible germ* (Definition 4.2 — a tuple carrying the four $G$-equations, the two slice divisibility families, the shift dictionary $h_5 = e$, and one of the two cap profiles). The two steps are

$$\text{counterexample}\ \overset{\text{Thm 4.3}}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{admissible germ}\ \overset{\text{Thm A}''}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{contradiction},$$

and the skeleton of the second is:

$$
\underbrace{\text{K-syzygy}}_{\S3} \Longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
e \equiv 0: & 2\Phi \equiv 0,\ \text{contradiction (one line)}\\[2pt]
e \not\equiv 0: & e \mid \Phi \Longrightarrow e = \gamma t^{a}\Pi,\ \Pi \mid q\ \text{squarefree}
\end{cases}
$$
then $\underbrace{a \ge 9}_{\S6} $ and $ \underbrace{a \le 9}_{\S7}$, hence $a = 9$; then the five cases $\deg\Pi = 0,1,2,3,4$ all die (§8).

Of the eleven machine steps, **none** is irreducibly machine work. Until commit `c519250` exactly one was — the marked-support feasibility test of §8.5 — and `support_certificates.py` (55/55) reduced it to five Bézout identities in `Z[y]`/`Z[p]` of degree ≤ 6 plus degree and valuation bookkeeping, hand-checkable at every one of the 40 `(k,z)` pairs (`SUPPORT_CERTIFICATES.md` §0). §4 is where the two objects are separated; it is short, and skipping it is how one ends up claiming a theorem one has not proved (§4 opening paragraph).

## 2. Conventions, and the imported setup

Throughout, $K$ has characteristic $0$; all statements below hold over a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra unless narrowed. Write
$$t := y+1 \in K[y].$$
**$t$ is a place of $K[y]$, not a coordinate.** (Helali's band coordinate $t = xy^2$ is a different object; the two must never be identified.) For $f \in K[y]$, $v_t(f)$ is the order of vanishing at $y=-1$.

### 2.1 The imported normalisation **[IMPORTED: GGHV22 §5; `PROOF_INVENTORY` C2, C3]**

At the $(8,28)$ corner one may normalise
$$\ell_{1,0}(P) = R^2,\qquad \ell_{1,0}(Q) = R^3,\qquad R = x^4C_4,\qquad C_4 = y^7(y+1) = y^7 t,$$
the same $C_4$ in **both** configurations (the shared corners $(8,14),(8,16)$ and $(12,21),(12,24)$ fix it). There is a Laurent series
$$C = \sum_{j \le 4} c_j x^j \in K[y,C_4^{-1}]((x^{-1})),\qquad P = C^2,$$
and — this is `[QQ1]`, the $\alpha$-strip WLOG, which we now state as an ordinary proposition rather than an opaque tag —

> **Proposition 2.1 (`[QQ1]`, the $\alpha$-strip normalisation).** Let $K$ have characteristic $0$, let $[P,Q] = x^2$, and grade by $v := v_{1,0}$ (the top $x$-degree valuation, i.e. $(\rho,\sigma) = (1,0)$, so $\rho+\sigma = 1$). Assume the $(8,28)$-corner data of the previous display: $v(P) = 8$, $v(Q) = 12$, $\ell(P) = R^2$, $\ell(Q) = R^3$ with $R = x^4C_4$ **primitive**, and $P = C^2$ with $v(C) = 4$. Then, after a bracket- and polygon-preserving change of the pair,
> $$Q = C^3 + \lambda C^{-1} + F,\qquad \lambda \in K,\qquad v_{1,0}(F) = -5 \ \text{ exactly}.$$

*Proof sketch, in five steps; the two imported inputs are named where they are used.*

1. **The leading forms commute.** GGV1 Prop 1.13 states $v([P,Q]) \le v(P)+v(Q)-(\rho+\sigma)$, **with equality iff $[\ell P,\ell Q] \ne 0$**. Here $v([P,Q]) = v(x^2) = 2$ while $v(P)+v(Q)-1 = 8+12-1 = 19$, a strict inequality, so $[\ell P,\ell Q] = 0$.
2. **Anything commuting with $\ell P$ is a pure power of $R$.** GGV1 Prop 2.1 ($P$ and $Q$ *aligned*): with $R$ primitive and $\ell P = R^2$, $[\ell P,\ell G] = 0$ forces $\ell(G) = \alpha_kR^k$ for a single $k$ and a scalar $\alpha_k$. Primitivity of $R = x^4y^7(y+1)$ is elementary (it is not $S^d$ for any $d\ge2$). The alignment exponents are forced: $3\cdot8 = 2\cdot12$ gives $(m,n) = (2,3)$ with $\gcd = 1$, whence $\ell P = R^2$, $\ell Q = R^3$.
3. **Stripping preserves the bracket.** $[C^k,C^2] = 0$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, so replacing $Q$ by $Q - \alpha_kC^k$ leaves $[\,\cdot\,,P]$ unchanged; the residual bracket stays $-x^2$ throughout the descent. Since $v(R) = v(C) = 4$, the strippable levels are exactly $v = 4k$, and the descent from $k=3$ downward has admissible range $k \in \{-1,0,1,2\}$.
4. **Termination at $-5$, and it is exact.** Let $F$ be the remainder once $\ell(F)$ is no longer a pure power. Then $[\ell F,\ell P]\ne0$, so Prop 1.13's **equality** case applies: $2 = v([F,P]) = v(F)+v(P)-1 = v(F)+7$, i.e. $v_{1,0}(F) = -5$ exactly. This is consistent, not assumed: $-5$ is not a multiple of $v_{1,0}(R) = 4$, so $\ell(F)$ *cannot* be any $\alpha_kR^k$ and the descent must halt exactly there.
5. **Clearing $\alpha_2,\alpha_1,\alpha_0$.** $\alpha_2$: replace $Q$ by $Q - \alpha_2P$. $\alpha_0$: replace $Q$ by $Q-\alpha_0$. $\alpha_1$: replace $P$ by $\widetilde P = P + \tfrac23\alpha_1$, so $\widetilde C = C + \tfrac13\alpha_1C^{-1}+\cdots$ and $\widetilde C^{\,3} = C^3 + \alpha_1C + \cdots$ — the $\alpha_1C$ term is absorbed into the cube, its residue landing in the $C^{-1}$ column, i.e. in $\lambda$. All three moves preserve $[P,Q] = x^2$ and both Newton polygons. The level $k=-1$ is *not* stripped: it is retained as $\lambda C^{-1}$. $\square$

Every finite arithmetic step above is machine-checked (`t6_premises_verify.py` P1a–P1e, P2a–P2e); GGV1 Props 1.13 and 2.1 are consumed **as stated**, which is exactly why the trust tier is **2/4** and not higher (§12.2). Here $v_{1,0}(F) = -5$ says $F$ has *no* term of $x$-degree above $-5$; and $C = x^4\cdot(\text{unit})$ gives $C^{-1}$ top degree $-4$. Both correction columns therefore start strictly below $M = -3$.

### 2.2 The forcing ODE and $\Phi$ **[PROVED, hand-checkable]**

The bracket relation $[P,Q]=x^2$, combined with the two leading forms, forces $f_1 := C_4^3F_{-5}$ to satisfy a first-order linear ODE:
$$8y(y+1)f_1' - 14(8y+7)f_1 = y^8(y+1)^2 .$$

> **Lemma 2.2.** The ODE has a *unique* polynomial solution,
> $$f_1 = -\frac{1}{6630}\,y^8(y+1)^2\,q(y),\qquad q(y) = 2048y^4 - 512y^3 + 320y^2 - 240y + 195 ,$$
> and $q$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, squarefree, with $\mathrm{disc}(q) = 12837954459480883200 \neq 0$, $q(-1) = 3315 = 3\cdot5\cdot13\cdot17 \ne 0$, and leading coefficient $2048$.

Uniqueness is linear algebra in degree $\le 14$; substitution is a one-line check. *(Verified independently for this paper: ODE residual exactly $0$; $q(-1)=3315$; $q$ irreducible.)* Set
$$\Phi := F_{-5}\,C_4^{31} = f_1 C_4^{28} = -\tfrac{1}{6630}\,y^{204}\,t^{30}\,q(y),$$
so $\deg\Phi = 238$, $\mathrm{ord}_y\Phi = 204$, $\mathrm{mult}_{y+1}\Phi = 30$. After stripping the $y$-power (§2.3) the operative object is
$$\Phi = -\tfrac{1}{6630}\,t^{30}\,q,\qquad \deg\Phi = 34 .$$

> **Lemma 2.3 (the exact valuation).** $v_t(\Phi) = 30$ **exactly**, and $[t^{30}]\Phi = -\tfrac12$.

*Proof.* $\Phi/t^{30} = -q/6630$, which at $y=-1$ equals $-3315/6630 = -1/2 \ne 0$. $\square$

**Why no cap or stratum can raise this to $\ge 30$**, which matters greatly in §7: $\Phi$ is a *fixed element of $\mathbb{Q}[y]$*. Its derivation consumes $[P,Q]=x^2$ and the two leading forms, both fixed by corners present in **both** configurations. No $d$-variable, no degree cap, no stratum, no field-scope toggle enters. The only way $v_t(\Phi)$ could exceed 30 is $q(-1) = 0$, and $q(-1) = 3315$.

### 2.3 The $D$-transform, stripping, and the $d_3$-killing shift **[IMPORTED / EXACT-CHECKED: `PROOF_INVENTORY` C5, C6; `window_caps_verify.py` W2/W3/W5]**

Put $D_j := c_j C_4^{7-2j}$, so $D_4 = 1$ and each $D_j \in K[y]$; and strip by the weight $12k$, $k = 4-j$:
$$d_j := D_j / y^{12(4-j)} .$$
The window bounds are $\mathrm{ord}_y D_j \ge 12k$ and $\deg_y D_j \le 14k$ — for the sub2 hull the slope 14 comes from the polygon alone, and $238/17 = 14$ agrees, two disjoint computations.

The shift
$$x \mapsto x - s,\qquad s = \frac{c_3}{4C_4} = \frac{D_3}{4C_4^2}$$
kills $d_3$. In $D$-coordinates it is, with **generalized** binomials and $\theta := -D_3/4$,
$$\widetilde D_j = \sum_{m \ge j}\binom{m}{m-j}\,D_m\,\theta^{\,m-j},\qquad D_4 = 1,$$
and $\binom{m}{m-j} = 0$ whenever $m \ge 0 > j$. **The shift is therefore triangular across zero: no non-negative $D_m$ feeds any spare.** In particular
$$\boxed{\widetilde D_{-1} = D_{-1}} \tag{2.3.1}$$
*exactly, with no $\theta$ at all.* This is a statement about index $-1$ specifically: $\widetilde D_{-2} = D_{-2} - \theta D_{-1}$ already mixes, and triangularity **fails** at index $-2$. That is why the shift cannot be stated as a clean change of coordinates and must be carried as a dictionary (§7.1).

⚠️ **$s$ is rational in $y$.** The shift is invertible on coefficient *sequences* but is not a change of variable preserving "comes from a polygon-supported polynomial". This is the source of both the positive-slice obstruction of §9 and of the C-2 finding of §1.3.

### 2.4 The G-system **[EXACT-CHECKED: `verify_derivation.py` (48 checks); `dm1_branch_verify.py` A1–A7]**

The G-system indeterminates are the **shifted stripped** coefficients (this identification is premise `[I3]`):

### 2.5 The two slice families **[IMPORTED: `[I1]`, `[I2]`; exponent arithmetic re-derived]**

Q_M = y^{2M-3}\,\frac{[u^{12-M}]H(u)^3}{t^{21-2M}} .$$
Both are polynomials and $\gcd(y,t)=1$. In level coordinates $n = 8-M$ resp. $n=12-M$, writing $p_n := [u^n]H^2$ and $r_n := [u^n]H^3$:

\qquad
\textbf{(Q)}\quad t^{2n-3} \mid r_n \ \ (n = 2,\dots,15).
\tag{2.5.1}
$$

### 2.6 The cap lemma **[PROVED here; `caps_audit.py`, 70/70, re-run for this paper]**

The degree caps are not a runtime read. They are a **theorem**, proved from the Newton supports, one valuation induction closed in $k$, and the $D$-transform — three ingredients, none of them longer than a paragraph — and a paper must state them that way — two of them (§1.4) have no margin at all, and a zero-margin input deferred to "the engine says so" is not an input a referee can check.

> **Lemma 2.5 (cap lemma).** Write $w := 4 - j$ for the $u$-weight of the symbol $d_j$, so $w(d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T) = (2,3,4,5,6,7,8)$. Then
> $$\deg_y d_j \;\le\; \lambda\,w,\qquad \lambda = \begin{cases}3 & \text{configuration (1)}\\ 2 & \text{configuration (2)}\end{cases}$$
> and each cap is **attained** by a generic instance, so none can be lowered.

*Proof.* Three ingredients, in order.

**(i) Three direction functionals, read off the polygons** — two for degrees, one for orders. For a linear form $\phi(i,j) = ai+bj$ the maximum over a lattice polygon is attained at a vertex, so the corner sets of §2.1 determine it. A brute-force hull scan gives, for the two configurations,
$$\max_{N(P)}(j-i) = 8 \ \ \text{(config (1))},\qquad \max_{N(P)}(j-2i) = 0 \ \ \text{(config (2))},\qquad \max_{N(P)}(2i-j) = 2 \ \ \text{(both)},$$
which translate into per-slice bounds $\deg_y P_i \le M_{\deg} - a\,i$ and $\mathrm{ord}_yP_i \ge 2i - M_{\mathrm{ord}}$ for all $i = 0..8$.

**(ii) One valuation induction, closed in $k$.** From $P = C^2$, the coefficient of $x^{8-k}$ reads
$$2C_4C_{4-k} + \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}C_{4-j}C_{4-(k-j)} = P_{8-k},\qquad\text{so}\qquad C_{4-k} = \frac{P_{8-k}-\sum_{j}C_{4-j}C_{4-(k-j)}}{2C_4}.$$
$\deg_y$ and $-\mathrm{ord}_y$ are valuations, so $v(A/B) = v(A)-v(B)$ exactly and $v(\sum) \le \max v$; the pivot $2C_4$ is never zero. Run the induction with hypothesis $v(C_{4-k}) \le h(k)$, where

$$h(k) = 8-k\ \ (\deg,\ \text{config (1)}),\qquad h(k) = 8-2k\ \ (\deg,\ \text{config (2)}),\qquad h(k) = 2k-7\ \ (v = -\mathrm{ord}_y,\ \text{both}).$$

Each closes **exactly**, as an identity in $k$: the product bound $h(j)+h(k-j)$ is **$j$-free** and equals the $P$-slice bound with no slack anywhere; $-v(C_4)+\text{slice} = h(k)$ identically in $k$ (with $v(C_4) = 8$, $8$, $-7$ respectively); and the base $k=1$, where the product is empty, gives $h(1)$. Hence, writing $j_x = 4-k$ for the $x$-exponent,
$$\deg_yC_{j_x} \le j_x+4 \ \text{(config (1))},\qquad \deg_yC_{j_x}\le 2j_x\ \text{(config (2))},\qquad \mathrm{ord}_yC_{j_x}\ge 2j_x-1\ \text{(both)}.$$

**(iii) The $D$-transform and the strip.** With $D_{j_x} = C_{j_x}C_4^{7-2j_x}$ and $\deg C_4 = 8$, $\mathrm{ord}\,C_4 = 7$:
$$\deg D_{j_x} \le (j_x{+}4) + 8(7{-}2j_x) = 60-15j_x,\qquad \deg D_{j_x}\le 2j_x + 8(7{-}2j_x) = 56-14j_x,$$
$$\mathrm{ord}\,D_{j_x} \ge (2j_x{-}1)+7(7{-}2j_x) = 48-12j_x .$$
At $j_x = 4-k$ these become $\deg \le 15k$ / $\le 14k$ and $\mathrm{ord} \ge 12k$ — the latter is exactly the window floor $y^{12k}$ of §2.3. Stripping by $y^{12k}$ subtracts the floor from the cap:
$$\lambda = 15-12 = 3\ \text{(config (1))},\qquad \lambda = 14-12 = 2\ \text{(config (2))}. \qquad\square$$

**The shift preserves every cap.** This matters, because the G-system indeterminates are the *shifted* coefficients. The shift is triangular with per-step slope $\mathrm{cap}(3)$, and $\mathrm{cap}(m) + (m-j)\cdot\mathrm{cap}(3) = \mathrm{cap}(j)$ holds identically in $(m,j)$ in all three directions — because $\mathrm{cap}(3)$ *is* the slope ($15$, $14$, $12$ respectively). So every term $\binom{m}{m-j}D_m\theta^{m-j}$ of $\widetilde D_j$ obeys the cap for $\widetilde D_j$, term by term.

Evaluating Lemma 2.5:

| | $d_2$ | $d_1$ | $d_0$ | $e$ | $R$ | $S$ | $T$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $u$-weight $w$ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| config (1) = sub1, $\lambda = 3$ | **6** | 9 | 12 | 15 | 18 | 21 | 24 |
| config (2) = sub2, $\lambda = 2$ | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | **12** | 14 | 16 |

The two boldface entries are the zero-margin ones of §1.4: $\deg d_2 \le 6$ drives the $k=0$ kill (§8.6) and $\deg R \le 12$ drives the configuration-(2) forcing $\deg e = 10$ (§9(b)). Both are now consequences of Lemma 2.5, not runtime reads. Two sensitivity facts belong with them. **Attainment is what forbids lowering a cap:** a generic series square root attains $\deg D_k = \lambda k$ and $\mathrm{ord}\,D_k = 12k$ exactly at every $k = 1..17$ in both configurations, so no cap can be silently tightened into an unearned proof — and lowering $\deg e$ to $9$ would empty configuration (2) outright, which attainment rules out. **Raising $\deg R$ is the dangerous direction:** it enters the configuration-(2) test as $2\deg R$, so an off-by-one costs two degrees at once.

## 3. The K-syzygy

### 3.1 The identity **[PROVED, hand-checkable in ~30 terms]**

> **Theorem 3.1 (K-syzygy).** With the generators (2.4.1),
> $$2\bigl(G_5 + d_2G_3 + d_1G_2 + d_0G_1\bigr) \;=\; 2\Phi \;-\; e\bigl(d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2\bigr)$$
> as an **exact** identity in $\mathbb{Z}[d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T,\Phi]$. Every $T$-term and every $d_0/d_1/d_2$ cross term cancels identically.

*Proof.* Expansion. *(Residual verified to be exactly $0$, independently, for this paper.)* $\square$

Write $K := 2\Phi - e(d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2)$ for the right-hand side, and
$$B := d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2 \qquad\text{(the \emph{bracket}).}$$

> **Corollary 3.2 (ideal equality).** $\langle G_1,G_2,G_3,G_5\rangle = \langle G_1,G_2,G_3,K\rangle$.

Indeed $K$ is an exact combination of the generators, and conversely $G_5 = K/2 - d_2G_3 - d_1G_2 - d_0G_1$. This is *equality*, not a necessary-condition weakening: the dense $G_5$ row may be replaced by the sparse four-term $K$ row with no saturation and no division by $e$.

The syzygy has been reached by three separate routes in this repository — as a term-list identity on the generators, as a power-series identity in the shifted coefficients, and (months earlier, from a different starting system) as the relation $2(H_5 + d_2H_3) = d_{-1}K_5$ in the $d_1=0$ generic-fibre lane, whose $K_5$ is literally this $K$.

### 3.2 The two branches

On a $G$-point all four generators vanish, so Theorem 3.1 gives
$$2\Phi = e\,\bigl(d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2\bigr) = e\,B \tag{3.2.1}$$
identically in $K[y]$. This single equation splits the problem, and the split is exhaustive by inspection.

### 3.3 The branch $e \equiv 0$ **[AUDITED: `dm1_branch_verify.py`, 28/28; verified independently here]**

> **Lemma 3.3.** No $G$-point has $e \equiv 0$. *(This is a statement about the bare four-equation object — the weakest hypothesis in the paper.)*

*Proof.* Put $e \equiv 0$ in (3.2.1): the right-hand side is $0$, so $2\Phi \equiv 0$, hence $\Phi \equiv 0$ whenever $2$ is invertible. But $\Phi = f_1C_4^{28} \neq 0$ by Lemma 2.2. $\square$

That is the whole proof — **one line**, with no case split and no use of $K[y]$ being a domain. Field scope: **$\mathrm{char} \ne 2$**, not $\mathrm{char}\,0$; reduced mod 2 the certificate degenerates ($2\Phi \equiv 0$ identically).

Two remarks worth recording. First, the previously recorded argument for this branch was a two-leg case split ($G_1|_{e=0} = 3RS \equiv 0$, so $R\equiv0$ or $S\equiv0$, each leg then forcing $\Phi\equiv0$) which needs a domain and a side condition; Lemma 3.3 is strictly stronger. Second, the leaf's recorded checker attribution in `PROOF_INVENTORY.md` (`[inferred: within verify_derivation.py]`) is **false** — `verify_derivation.py` runs 48 checks over the derivation and contains no $e=0$ substitution anywhere. Before `dm1_branch_verify.py` there was no checker for it. The mathematics was nonetheless sound; it had been discharged by accident, three times over, by lanes consuming Theorem 3.1 for other purposes.

Lemma 3.3 also *explains* why $e$ is the obstruction: $2\Phi$ is congruent to an explicit multiple of $e$ modulo the ideal, which is the structural source of the $d_{-1}^{21}$ factor in the master identity $f_{31}\cdot f_{37}\cdot d_{-1}^{21}\equiv 0$ that the older elimination route produced.

### 3.4 The branch $e \not\equiv 0$: $e \mid \Phi$ **[EXACT-CHECKED ×3: `divisor_syzygy.py` C1–C4, 7/7; and (D1), (D2) additionally PROVED rather than imported, at `spine9_audit.py` A13–A14]**

> **Theorem 3.4.** On every $G$-point with $e \not\equiv 0$, $\;e \mid \Phi$. Consequently, over $\overline K$,
> $$e = \gamma\, t^{a}\,\Pi,\qquad \gamma \in K^\times,\quad a = a_t = v_t(e),\quad \Pi \mid q \text{ squarefree},\quad k := \deg\Pi \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}.$$
> In particular: **(D1)** every root of $e$ lies in $\{-1\} \cup \{\text{roots of } q\}$ — the off-support factor is a unit; **(D2)** every root of $q$ divides $e$ to order **at most 1**, i.e. the exponents $b_i \in \{0,1\}$; and $\deg e = a + \sum b_i = a + k$.

*Proof.* (3.2.1) with $e \ne 0$ gives $e \mid 2\Phi$, and $2$ is invertible. By Lemma 2.2, $\Phi = -\tfrac{1}{6630}t^{30}q$ with $q$ squarefree and $q(-1) \ne 0$, so its radical is $t\cdot q$ and every simple root of $q$ occurs to order 1. $\square$

Three things make Theorem 3.4 the right shape. It uses **no** degree stratum, **no** saturation, **no** $d_1 = 0$, and **no** genericity: it holds on the whole variety $V(G_1,G_2,G_3,G_5)$. It is **not monotone in degree** — it deletes whole support cells, top stratum included. And it is *exactly* the statement that turns the problem into the five-case analysis of §8.

One correction that must travel with (D2): $\deg e = a + \sum b_i$ is **not** automatic from the ambient enumeration. The state artifacts record $3844$ configurations with $\deg e \ne a + \sum b_i$ — defect-1 $e$ carrying a free extra root. It is (D1) that repairs this, by confining every root of $e$ to $\{-1,r_1,\dots,r_4\}$ and forcing defect $0$. The consequence survives, but it needs that extra step.

### 3.5 A second syzygy: $6WZ = e^5$ **[PROVED, hand-checkable; `toric_syzygy.py` B1–B3, with seven coefficient mutations]**

The K-syzygy is not the only relation the four generators hide. There is a second one, on three rows rather than four, and it turns out to be the object of which §8's cofactor identity is a normalised shadow (§8.3).

Arrange the last four coordinates in the $2\times3$ Hankel matrix
$$\mathcal H \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} e & R & S\\ R & S & T\end{pmatrix},$$
whose three $2\times2$ minors are $W := eS - R^2$, $Z := eT - RS$, and $RT - S^2$. (The vanishing of all three cuts out the cone over the twisted cubic — a toric variety; hence the name below. Only the first two minors occur.)

> **Theorem 3.5 (toric syzygy).** In $\mathbb{Z}[d_0,d_1,d_2,e,R,S,T]$,
> $$2e^2G_3 \;-\; 4eR\,G_2 \;+\; 2R^2G_1 \;=\; 6\,W\,Z \;-\; e^5 ,$$
> residual exactly $0$. Consequently **every** $G$-point satisfies
> $$\boxed{\,6\,W\,Z \;=\; e^5\,}\tag{3.5.1}$$

*Proof.* Expansion; $\sim40$ terms, and every $d_0,d_1,d_2$ cross term cancels. $\square$

Four things to say about what (3.5.1) costs, because the answer is *nothing*.

* **It does not use $G_5$, and therefore does not use $\Phi$.** $G_5$ is the only generator in which $\Phi$ occurs; the combination above is built from $G_1,G_2,G_3$ alone. So (3.5.1) is insensitive to the entire content of §2.2 — it would hold verbatim for any other forcing function.
* **It uses no cap, no slice divisibility, no branch condition, no support enumeration, and no degree stratum.** Like Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4, it is a statement about the bare four-equation object of Definition 4.1, and in fact about a three-equation subobject of it.
* **It is genuinely a relation, not a rearrangement.** Corrupting any one of the five coefficients $(2,-4,2,6,1)$, or the exponent $5$, leaves a nonzero residual; $W$ and $Z$ cannot be exchanged.
* **Combined with $e\mid S$ (Lemma 11.5) it sharpens.** Writing $S = e\bar S$ makes $Z = e Z_1$ with $Z_1 = T - R\bar S$, and (3.5.1) becomes, on any $G$-point with $e\ne0$,
  $$6\,W\,Z_1 \;=\; e^4. \tag{3.5.2}$$
  Since $\overline K[y]$ is a UFD this is a **divisor law**: at every place $\beta$,
  $$v_\beta(W) + v_\beta(Z_1) \;=\; 4\,v_\beta(e). \tag{3.5.3}$$
  §8.4 spends exactly this.

One immediate consequence deserves its own line, because it is what makes §4.2a's chart cover *everything*.

> **Corollary 3.6.** On every $G$-point, $e \not\equiv 0$ **and** $W = eS - R^2 \not\equiv 0$.

*Proof.* $e\not\equiv0$ is Lemma 3.3. If $W \equiv 0$ then $e^5 = 6WZ = 0$ by (3.5.1), so $e\equiv0$ — contradiction. $\square$

Note what this costs: Lemma 3.3 (one line, char $\ne2$) plus Theorem 3.5 (one expansion). No cap, no slice family, no branch. It says the two denominators of the chart of Proposition 4.4 never vanish, so that chart is not a chart at all — it is the whole variety.

The companion relation of §10, $W^2 = R^4 + d_2e^2R^2 + d_1e^3R + d_0e^4$, is the *other* identity carried by $W$; its cofactors are explicit too, $W^2 - \bigl(R^4+d_2e^2R^2+d_1e^3R+d_0e^4\bigr) = \tfrac23\bigl(e^2G_2 - eR\,G_1\bigr)$, a two-row syzygy. So $W$ satisfies one quadratic and one multiplicative constraint, and only the second is used below.

## 4. Two objects: $G$-points and admissible germs

This section exists because an earlier draft of this paper got its own theorem statement wrong, in a way worth being explicit about. It defined a *germ* as a bare solution of $G_1=G_2=G_3=G_5=0$ and then asserted "there is no germ" — but the proof below also consumes the $P$- and $Q$-slice divisibility families (§2.5), the *unshifted* coefficients $h_i$ and the shift dictionary $h_5 = e$ (§§2.3, 7.1), and one of the two cap profiles of Lemma 2.5. **None of that is implied by the four equations.** So the statement was strictly stronger than the argument. Nothing in the exclusion is wrong — every hypothetical counterexample does produce all of that structure — but the *scope* was misdeclared, and this is exactly the distinction a Lean formalisation would refuse to let pass.

The repair is to name two objects and keep them apart.

### 4.1 The definitions

> **Definition 4.1 ($G$-point, a.k.a. algebraic germ).** A **$G$-point** is a tuple $(d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T) \in K[y]^7$ with
> $$G_1 = G_2 = G_3 = G_5 = 0,$$
> the generators being (2.4.1) with the fixed $\Phi$ of §2.2. Nothing else is assumed: no divisibility family, no cap, no relation to any $h_i$, no configuration.

> **Definition 4.2 (admissible germ).** An **admissible germ** is a tuple
> $$\bigl(d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T,\;H,\;\mathfrak{c}\bigr),\qquad H(u) = \textstyle\sum_{k\ge0}h_ku^k,\ h_0 = 1,\ \mathfrak{c}\in\{(1),(2)\},$$
> satisfying **all four** of the following.
>
> **(A1) The $G$-equations.** $(d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T)$ is a $G$-point.
> **(A2) The slice divisibilities.** With $p_n := [u^n]H^2$ and $r_n := [u^n]H^3$,
> $$t^{2n-2}\mid p_n\ (n = 2..8),\quad p_n = 0\ (n\ge9),\quad t^{2n-3}\mid r_n\ (n=2..15).$$
> **(A3) The shift dictionary.** The relations (7.1.1) hold between the $h_i$ and $(d_2,d_1,e,R,S)$ — in particular $\boxed{h_5 = e}$ — with $\theta = -h_1/4$ and generalized binomials as in §2.3.
> **(A4) A cap profile.** $\deg_yd_j \le \lambda(\mathfrak c)\,w(d_j)$ for every $j$, with $\lambda((1)) = 3$, $\lambda((2)) = 2$ as in Lemma 2.5.

Both objects live over an arbitrary field of characteristic $0$; (A2)–(A4) are what a $G$-point has no reason to satisfy, and they are precisely what the argument below uses beyond (A1).

### 4.2 The transfer, and the top-level logic

> **Theorem 4.3 (transfer).** Let $(P,Q)$ be a pair as in Theorem A — either configuration. Then $(P,Q)$ determines an admissible germ, in its own configuration $\mathfrak c$.

*Proof.* (A1): §2.1 normalises, §2.3 strips and shifts, §2.4 selects the twelve non-vacuous equations and runs the linear phase; Lemma 2.4 makes that phase an **equivalence**, with unit pivot $2$ and no variable inverted, so the four generators vanish. (A2): Proposition 2.1 gives $Q_M = (C^3)_M$ for every $M \ge -3$, and $P = C^2$ unconditionally; the slice formulas of §2.5 then say exactly that $P_M$ and $Q_M$ are polynomials, which in level coordinates is (2.5.1). The strengthening $p_n = 0$ for $n \ge 9$ is that $P$ has no negative $x$-powers. (A3) is the content of §2.3's generalized-binomial shift together with the triangularity identity (2.3.1), which gives $h_5 = \widetilde D_{-1} = D_{-1} = e$; this identification is premise `[I3]`. (A4) is Lemma 2.5, whose cap survives the shift term by term. $\square$

The top-level logic of the paper is therefore a two-step, and we state it that way:

$$\boxed{\ \text{counterexample in configuration (1) or (2)} \ \overset{\text{Thm 4.3}}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{admissible germ} \ \overset{\text{Thm A}''}{\Longrightarrow}\ \text{contradiction.}\ }$$

> **Theorem A$''$.** There is no admissible germ, in either configuration.

Theorem A$''$ is what §§3–9 prove: Theorem 8.6 for $\mathfrak c = (1)$, Theorem 9.1 for $\mathfrak c = (2)$. It implies Theorem A by Theorem 4.3. What earlier drafts called "Theorem A$'$ — there is no germ" is **not** proved here, and — this is the point of the next proposition — it is not merely unproved but **false**.

### 4.2a The bare variety is nonempty, and we can describe it **[PROVED, hand-checkable; `toric_syzygy.py` A1–A4, E1–E5, with mutation controls]**

The bare variety is not merely nonempty; it is a *rational hypersurface*, and the hypersurface is the K-syzygy. By Corollary 3.6 the locus below is not a chart but the whole of it.

> **Proposition 4.4 (the chart, which is everything).** On the locus $e \ne 0$, $W := eS - R^2 \ne 0$ — which by Corollary 3.6 contains **every** $G$-point — the three equations $G_1 = G_2 = G_3 = 0$ determine $(d_0,d_1,T)$ **uniquely** in terms of the free data $(d_2,e,R,S)$:
> $$T = \frac{e^4}{6W} + \frac{RS}{e},\qquad
> d_1 = -\frac{2d_2R}{e} - \frac{4RS}{e^2} - \frac{e^3}{3W},\qquad
> d_0 = \frac{d_2R^2}{e^2} + \frac{S(eS+2R^2)}{e^3} + \frac{e^2R}{3W},$$
> and after this substitution the remaining equation $G_5 = 0$ becomes **exactly** the K-syzygy of Theorem 3.1,
> $$2\Phi \;=\; e\bigl(d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2\bigr). \tag{4.4.1}$$
> So $V(G_1,G_2,G_3,G_5)$ is, **in its entirety**, the graph over the hypersurface (4.4.1) in the four free coordinates $(d_2,e,R,S)$ of three explicit rational functions.

*Proof.* Linear algebra: $G_1,G_2,G_3$ are affine-linear in $(d_0,d_1,T)$ with determinant $\tfrac{27}{4}e^3W$. Substituting and clearing denominators gives (4.4.1); residual exactly $0$. $\square$

Read the $T$-formula again: it says $eT - RS = e^5/(6W)$, i.e. $Z = e^5/(6W)$, i.e.
$$6\,W\,Z = e^5 .$$
**Theorem 3.5 is the $T$-formula of this chart.** That is the structural reason the toric syzygy costs nothing: solving $G_1$ for $T$ *is* the syzygy, and $G_2,G_3$ are what make the solution consistent.

Two explicit polynomial families fall out, and both matter.

> **Corollary 4.5 (two families of $G$-points).** Each of the following is a $G$-point in the sense of Definition 4.1 — all four generators vanish identically, with the **fixed** $\Phi$ of §2.2.
>
> **(a) The $R = 0$ family.** For $E \in \overline K[y]$ nonzero with $E^3 \mid 2\Phi$,
> $$\bigl(d_2,d_1,d_0,e,R,S,T\bigr) = \Bigl(\tfrac{2\Phi}{E^3} - 3,\ -\tfrac{E}{3},\ 1,\ E,\ 0,\ E,\ \tfrac{E^2}{6}\Bigr),$$
> and the divisibility hypothesis holds **exactly** for $E = \lambda t^m$ with $\lambda \in \overline K^\times$, $0\le m\le10$.
>
> **(b) The unit-$W$ family.** For any $\lambda,w \in \overline K^\times$ and **any** $A \in \overline K[y]$, take
> $$e = \lambda,\qquad R = A,\qquad S = \frac{A^2+w}{\lambda},\qquad d_2 = \frac{1}{\lambda^2}\Bigl(\frac{2\Phi}{\lambda} - 6A^2 - 3w\Bigr),$$
> with $(d_0,d_1,T)$ from Proposition 4.4. Then $W = w$ is a unit, every denominator in the chart is a unit, and all seven coordinates are polynomials.

*Proof.* (a) Substitute. With $R = 0$: $G_1 = \tfrac32d_1e^2+3eT = -\tfrac12E^3+\tfrac12E^3 = 0$; $G_2 = -\tfrac32d_0e^2+\tfrac32S^2 = 0$; $G_3 = -\tfrac12e^3+3ST = 0$; and $G_{5\mathrm{body}} = -\tfrac12d_2E^3 - \tfrac32E^3 = -\Phi$. For the classification of $E$: $d_2 \in \overline K[y]$ forces $E^3\mid2\Phi$, and by Lemma 2.2 $2\Phi = 2c\,t^{30}q$ with $q$ squarefree, $q(-1)\ne0$; an irreducible $p\mid E$ with $p\nmid t$ would give $v_p(2\Phi) = 1 < 3$. Hence $E = \lambda t^m$, $3m\le30$.
(b) $W = \lambda\cdot\frac{A^2+w}{\lambda} - A^2 = w$; the chart's denominators are $e = \lambda$ and $W = w$, both units, and (4.4.1) is exactly how $d_2$ was defined. $\square$

Three remarks, all of which matter more than the corollary.

**(i) The $G$-point / admissible-germ distinction is mathematically essential, not bookkeeping.** §4's split was introduced as a *scope repair* — an earlier draft overclaimed, and the honest fix was to name two objects. Proposition 4.4 upgrades that from hygiene to necessity. The four equations alone are satisfied by a family with a **free polynomial parameter** $A$ of unbounded degree; no argument consuming only (A1) can ever close the problem, and there is no clever strengthening of the four equations to be found, because on the chart they are *equivalent* to one hypersurface equation. The gap between Definition 4.1 and Definition 4.2 is where the entire proof lives, and it has now been measured rather than asserted.

**(ii) Which hypothesis excludes these families — and for (a) the naive answer is wrong.** It is tempting to say of family (a) that "$d_2 = 2\Phi/E^3-3$ has degree $34$, annihilating the cap $\deg d_2\le6$ of Lemma 2.5", and for $m=0$ that is right. But $\deg d_2 = 34-3m$, so the $d_2$ cap is violated **only for $m\le9$**; at $m = 10$, $\deg d_2 = 4$ sits comfortably inside both cap profiles. What kills $m=10$ is a different coordinate. So hypothesis **(A4) alone excludes both families, in three cases**:

| | binding cap, config (1) $\lambda=3$ | binding cap, config (2) $\lambda=2$ |
|---|---|---|
| family (a), $m\le9$ | $\deg d_2 = 34-3m \ge 7 > 6$ | $\ge 7 > 4$ |
| family (a), $m = 10$ | $\deg d_1 = 10 > 9$ | $\deg d_1 = 10 > 6$; also $\deg T = 20>16$ |
| family (b), any $A$ | $\deg d_2 \ge 16 > 6$ | $\deg d_2 \ge 16 > 4$ |

The bound $\deg d_2\ge16$ in family (b) is sharp and is *not* the obvious $34$: if $\deg A \ne 17$ then $\deg(2\Phi/\lambda - 6A^2) \ge 34$, while $\deg A = 17$ permits cancellation, and matching the top eighteen coefficients — a triangular system with two sign branches — leaves degree exactly $16$ on both. The cap test is not vacuous either: raising $\lambda$ to $4$ lets family (a)'s $m = 9,10$ through. So the exclusion is a real consequence of the *values* $\lambda = 3,2$ that Lemma 2.5 derives, and it would survive a cap loosened as far as $15$ but not as far as $16$.

**(iii) This is genuinely stronger than the point previously cited here.** §10 records a point with $R=0$, $e\ne0$, $\Phi\ne0$, $d_1\ne0$, namely $e=1,R=0,S=1,T=\tfrac16,d_0=1,d_1=-\tfrac13,d_2=0,\Phi=\tfrac32$. That is family (a)'s $(\lambda,m) = (1,0)$ member **with $\Phi$ treated as a free indeterminate and solved for** — the right reading for §10's own purpose, where the ambient ring is $\mathbb{Q}[d_0,\dots,T,\Phi]$ and the conclusion is an ideal non-membership. It is **not** a $G$-point in the sense of Definition 4.1, because Definition 4.1 fixes $\Phi$ to the degree-$34$ polynomial of §2.2 while $\Phi = \tfrac32$ is a constant: substituting that tuple with the fixed $\Phi$ leaves $G_5 = \Phi - \tfrac32 \ne 0$. Corollary 4.5(a)'s $(\lambda,m)=(1,0)$ member is the repair, and it carries $d_2 = 2\Phi-3$, not $d_2 = 0$. Earlier drafts of this section conflated the two, and the parenthetical that stood here asserted the emptiness question was *open* while pointing at a purported point of the variety — a self-contradiction either way round.

## 5. The stacked positive-slice obstruction

This section is the technical heart of the lower bound, and its point is a counting fact that is easy to get wrong.

### 5.1 Each family alone is vacuous **[PROVED; `slice_obstruction_basis.py` S4]**

Since $h_0 = 1$,
$$p_n = 2h_n + q_n^P,\qquad r_n = 3h_n + q_n^Q,$$
where $q_n^P, q_n^Q$ involve only $h_1,\dots,h_{n-1}$. In both configurations the degree freedom of $h_n$ exceeds $2n-2$, so **$h_n$ surjects onto the forbidden $P$-jets, and separately onto the forbidden $Q$-jets.** Hence

| level $n$ | $\mathrm{coker}(P\text{ only})$ | $\mathrm{coker}(Q\text{ only})$ | naive sum | **stacked** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **1** |
| 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **3** |
| 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **5** |
| 5..8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **7, 9, 11, 13** |

A free fresh coefficient absorbs either support map completely. **Counting the two sides separately reports zero and misses everything.**

### 5.2 The stacked family **[PROVED, hand-checkable]**

Stacked, the *same* fresh coefficient must serve both, and because the diagonal entries are $L^P_n = 2$ and $L^Q_n = 3$,
$$2\cdot 3 - 3\cdot 2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h_n \text{ cancels identically in } 2r_n - 3p_n .$$
With $K := H - 1$ one has the elementary identity $2H^3 - 3H^2 = -1 + 3K^2 + 2K^3$, so the canonical basis of new obstructions at level $n$ is the set of $2n-3$ jets of

$$\boxed{\;t^{2n-3} \;\Big|\; [u^n]\bigl(3K^2 + 2K^3\bigr)\;},\qquad K = H-1 . \tag{5.2.1}$$

involving $h_1,\dots,h_{n-1}$ **only**. This is the entire new content, and it is independent of the cell, the branch, and the configuration.

*Cheapest instance.* At $n=2$: $[u^2](3K^2+2K^3) = 3h_1^2$, so $t \mid h_1$, i.e. $d_3(-1) = 0$. Concretely $P_6 = y^{10}(2d_2+d_3^2)/t^2$ and $Q_{10} = 3y^{17}(d_2+d_3^2)/t$ are both polynomials; subtracting gives $t \mid d_3^2$. The $P$-column alone leaves $h_1(-1)$ free; the $Q$-column pins it.

### 5.3 Controls, and the one weak joint **[`slice_obstruction_basis.py` S6a–S6c]**

Three controls are non-negotiable here and all pass. **S6a**: with the corners loaded from the transcribed source and $P_8 = C_4^2$ forced, all nine slices $P_0..P_8$ reproduce exactly and every $P$-condition holds identically. **S6b (the joint control)**: $C = x^4C_4 + ay^5x^3 + by^3x^2 + cyx + e_0$ is a Laurent *polynomial*, so $P=C^2$, $Q=C^3$ are honest polynomials with $\lambda = F = 0$ — a genuine point of the slice system — and on it **every** obstruction functional, $P$-side, $Q$-side and stacked, vanishes identically at every level $2..8$. **S6c (mutation)**: perturbing the single coefficient $h_1$ by $+1$ makes the predicted level-2 stacked functional go nonzero. The obstruction fires on the mutation and on nothing else.

**Stated limitation.** The joint control has $h_5 = \dots = h_8 = 0$: any $C$ finite in $x$ has zero spares, and any $C$ with nonzero spares is an infinite series whose $Q$-conditions are exactly what is at issue. So the level-10 step — the one that bounds $e$ — is controlled *structurally* (the forced profile satisfies it; the fresh coefficient enters linearly with a unit coefficient) but **not** by an instance with $e \ne 0$. After `[QQ1]`, this is the weakest joint in the lower bound.

## 6. The cascade, and $a_t \ge 9$

### 6.1 The cascade **[AUDITED: `slice_obstruction_audit.py`, 56/56, CONFIRMED-WITH-CORRECTIONS; recomputed from scratch in `syzygy_collision.py` X7 and `at_le9_audit.py` D0–D15]**

Every $P$-condition is absorbable, so write
$$h_n = -\tfrac12 q_n^P + t^{2n-2}g_n \quad (n \le 8),\qquad h_n = -\tfrac12 q_n^P \quad (n \ge 9),\qquad g_n \text{ free}. \tag{6.1.1}$$
Then every $P$-condition holds identically and the only remaining content is (5.2.1). Running it level by level:

| level $n$ | required | lowest nonzero jet | forced factor | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | $t^1$ | $t^0$ | $3g_{1,0}^2$ | $v_t(h_1) \ge 1$ |
| 4 | $t^5$ | $t^4$ | $\tfrac34(g_{1,1}^2-2g_{2,0})^2$ | $v_t(h_2) \ge 3$ |
| 6 | $t^9$ | $t^8$ | $3(\cdots)^2$ | $v_t(h_3) \ge 5$ |
| 8 | $t^{13}$ | $t^{12}$ | $\tfrac34(\cdots - 2g_{4,0})^2$ | $v_t(h_4) \ge 7$ |
| **10** | $t^{17}$ | $t^{16}$ | $3(\cdots + g_{5,0})^2$ | $\mathbf{v_t(h_5) \ge 9}$ |
| **12** | $t^{21}$ | $t^{20}$ | $(\cdots - 2g_{6,0})^2$ | $\mathbf{v_t(h_6) \ge 11}$ |
| 3,5,7,9,11 | — | all vanish identically | — | nothing |

Uniformly: **level $2m$ forces the $t^{2m-2}$ coefficient of $h_m$ to vanish**, advancing $v_t(h_m)$ from $2m-2$ to $2m-1$. Every firing jet is a **perfect square** — multiplicity exactly 2 — linear in one fresh $g$-coefficient with a *constant* leading coefficient, so every step is a forced consequence and never a choice of component. Odd levels contribute nothing.

**The ordering is load-bearing.** Before reduction by the relations already derived, the level-12 jet factors as $A_6\cdot(2A_2A_4+A_6)$ — **two coprime non-constant factors**, which would branch. Modulo the relation $A_2A_6 = 0$ (itself from (P<) at $n=8$) it becomes $\tfrac32A_6^2$, a perfect square. The perfect-square property is a property of the *correctly ordered* deduction, not of the level. The same phenomenon occurs one level down. This is why §5–§6 cannot be compressed into a clean change of coordinates, and why the ~6 pages they occupy are irreducible.

**The cascade constrains; it does not contradict.** Under $h_k = t^{2k-1}(\text{free})$, the substitution $u = v/t^2$ gives $K = \widehat H(v)/t$ and
$$[u^n](3K^2+2K^3) = 3t^{2n-2}[v^n]\widehat H^2 + 2t^{2n-3}[v^n]\widehat H^3,$$
divisible by $t^{2n-3}$ for **every** $n$. So the forced profile satisfies all stacked conditions identically. The bounds are also **sharp**: the joint control of §5.3 attains $v_t(h_k) = 2k-1$ exactly for $k = 1..4$.

### 6.2 The profile

Recording the levels actually used, together with the two rows derived from (6.1.1) rather than from a cascade level:

$$v_t(h_1)\ge1,\quad v_t(h_2)\ge3,\quad v_t(h_3)\ge5,\quad v_t(h_4)\ge7,\quad v_t(h_5)\ge9,\quad v_t(h_6)\ge11,\quad v_t(h_7)\ge12,\quad v_t(h_8)\ge13. \tag{6.2.1}$$

> **Lemma 6.1.** $v_t(h_6)\ge10$, $v_t(h_7)\ge11$, $v_t(h_8)\ge12$ follow from the **audited** rows $v_t(h_{1..5}) \ge (1,3,5,7,9)$ and (6.1.1) alone, with no cascade level beyond 10:
> $$v_t(h_6)\ge\min(1{+}9,\,3{+}7,\,5{+}5,\,10) = 10,\quad v_t(h_7)\ge\min(1{+}10,\,3{+}9,\,5{+}7,\,12)=11,\quad v_t(h_8)\ge12 .$$
> Once level 12 supplies $v_t(h_6)\ge11$, the same condition upgrades to $v_t(h_7)\ge12$, $v_t(h_8)\ge13$.

So levels 14 and 16 are **not needed** anywhere, and the $h_7$ row is not an unaudited independent input.

### 6.3 $a_t \ge 9$

> **Theorem 6.2.** On every admissible germ, $a_t \ge 9$ — in both configurations, on both branches.

*Proof.* By (A3) and (2.3.1), $h_5 = \widetilde D_{-1} = D_{-1} = e$ **exactly**, so $v_t(e) = v_t(h_5) \ge 9$ by level 10. $\square$

Both hypotheses this consumes are declared in Definition 4.2 and both are worth attacking: (A2), which exists only because of Proposition 2.1 (`[QQ1]`) — without it the $Q$ column does not exist at all — and (A3), the identification $h_5 = e$ (premise `[I3]`), without which the cascade is a statement about unshifted coefficients and constrains nothing. Both are also delivered by Theorem 4.3 from the same normalisation that delivers the G-system itself: if `[QQ1]` fell, (A1) would fall with (A2), not merely this theorem.

## 7. The bracket collapse, and $a_t \le 9$

The lower bound is a statement about coefficients the cascade bounds directly. The upper bound needs the *same* coefficients, which means pushing the bracket $B$ out of the shifted chart it is written in and into the unshifted one.

### 7.1 The dictionary **[AUDITED: `at_le9_audit.py` C1–C9; `syzygy_collision.py` X4–X5; `spine9_audit.py` G1]**

With generalized binomials and $\theta = -h_1/4$:
$$
d_2 = h_2 - \tfrac38h_1^2,\qquad
d_1 = h_3 - \tfrac12h_1h_2 + \tfrac18h_1^3,\qquad
\boxed{e = h_5},
$$
$$
R = h_6 + \tfrac14 h_1h_5,\qquad
S = h_7 + \tfrac12 h_1h_6 + \tfrac1{16}h_1^2h_5 . \tag{7.1.1}
$$

Three guards, because getting this wrong is the easiest route to a false kill.

* **Triangularity is about $\theta$-independence, and nothing more.** With $\theta$ held independent, neither $\widetilde D_{-2}$ nor $\widetilde D_{-3}$ contains $h_1$; once $\theta = -h_1/4$ is substituted, $h_1$ reappears in **both**. Triangularity protects $\widetilde D_{-1} = D_{-1}$ *completely* and nothing else. The two halves are checked separately so they cannot be conflated.
* **Form-invariance.** The dictionary is invariant under the stripping $d_j = D_j/y^{12(4-j)}$, so it holds verbatim in the stripped coordinates both the G-system and the slice calculus use; and $y$ is a unit at $t$, so no valuation moves.
* **Mutation.** Dropping the mixing (pretending $d_2=h_2$, $R=h_6$, $S=h_7$) gives a *different* bracket, differing by $-3h_1h_5h_6$. The collapse below is a real computation, not an identity any dictionary satisfies.

An independent mechanism reproduces (7.1.1): the generating function $\widetilde H(u) = (1-\alpha u)^4H(u/(1-\alpha u))$ with $\alpha = h_1/4$ gives all of $\widetilde h_1,\dots,\widetilde h_7$ identically. And one generalized-binomial model reproduces **all five** relations (7.1.1) simultaneously with residual $0$ — a five-for-one fit, which is itself evidence that the convention is being applied rather than fitted per object.

### 7.2 The collapse **[PROVED, and trivially hand-checkable]**

> **Theorem 7.1 (bracket collapse).** Substituting (7.1.1),
> $$d_2e^2 + 3eS + 3R^2 \;=\; h_2h_5^2 \;+\; 3h_5h_7 \;+\; 3h_1h_5h_6 \;+\; 3h_6^2 .$$

*Proof.* The $h_1^2h_5^2$ contributions of the three summands are $-\tfrac38$, $+\tfrac3{16}$, $+\tfrac3{16}$, which sum to $0$. Everything else is immediate. $\square$ *(Verified independently for this paper; residual exactly 0.)*

Four terms, every one a monomial in coefficients (6.2.1) bounds directly. The decisive point: the $3h_6^2$ term — which the shifted form hides inside $R$, and which any inverse-shift bound can only reach through $v_t(R) \ge \min(v_t(h_6),\,1+a_t)$ — is now **exposed with no $a_t$ in it at all**. *That is what moves the bound from 10 to 9.*

### 7.3 The collision **[AUDITED: `at_le9_audit.py` E4–E9, 76/76; `syzygy_collision.py` X9–X14, 25/25]**

By (3.2.1), $v_t(\Phi) = v_t(e) + v_t(B)$; by Lemma 2.3 the left side is **exactly** 30; so

$$\boxed{\;a_t + v_t(B) = 30\;}\qquad\text{an \textbf{equality}, not a bound.} \tag{7.3.1}$$

That exactness is the whole engine: it converts *lower* bounds on the terms of $B$ into a contradiction. With (6.2.1) and $v_t(h_5) = a_t$:

| $a_t$ | need $v_t(B)$ | $h_2h_5^2$ | $3h_5h_7$ | $3h_1h_5h_6$ | $3h_6^2$ | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8 | 22 | $\ge19$ | $\ge20$ | $\ge20$ | $\ge22$ | survives |
| **9** | **21** | **$\ge21$** | **$\ge21$** | **$\ge21$** | $\ge22$ | **survives** |
| **10** | **20** | $\ge23$ | $\ge22$ | $\ge22$ | $\ge22$ | **REFUTED** |
| 11 | 19 | $\ge25$ | $\ge23$ | $\ge23$ | $\ge22$ | REFUTED |
| 12 | 18 | $\ge27$ | $\ge24$ | $\ge24$ | $\ge22$ | REFUTED |
| 14 | 16 | $\ge31$ | $\ge26$ | $\ge26$ | $\ge22$ | REFUTED |

In closed form the three binding inequalities are
$$3+2a > 30-a \iff a\ge10,\qquad a+12 > 30-a \iff a\ge10,\qquad 22 > 30-a \iff a\ge9 .$$

> **Theorem 7.2.** On every admissible germ, $a_t \le 9$ — cap-free, branch-free, configuration-independent.

*Proof.* For $a_t \ge 10$ every term of $B$ has $v_t > 30 - a_t$, so $v_t(B) > 30-a_t$, contradicting (7.3.1). $\square$

Checked for $a_t = 10,\dots,59$. **At $a_t = 9$ three of the four terms sit on exactly $21 = 30-9$: the minimum can be attained and there is no contradiction**, and this survival is asserted as a check, so a bug that refuted everything would fail the suite. The bound is a threshold, not a blanket — which is the shape a correct result has and a blanket bug does not.

**What is load-bearing, measured.** Cascade level 12 is the single new input and it has **zero margin**: with only the level-10 profile ($v_t(h_6)\ge10$) the $3h_6^2$ term lands on exactly 20 and $a_t = 10$ **survives**. Running the checker with the cascade truncated at level 10 makes the kill genuinely fail. Conversely $v_t(h_1)\ge1$ is consumed by *nothing* in the collision — it is needed only to start the cascade — and $v_t(h_2)\ge3$ has slack 2. $v_t(\Phi) = 30$ tolerates $+1$ but nothing downward.

**What this argument does not use**, worth stating because it shrinks the premise set relative to every neighbouring result: not $e \mid S$; not "$d_2$ is a polynomial"; not the place trichotomy; **no degree cap at all**; no stratum premise, no branch split, no configuration split; no cascade level 14 or 16.

### 7.4 $a_t = 9$, and the alternate regime

> **Theorem 7.3.** On every admissible germ, $a_t = 9$ exactly.

Theorems 6.2 and 7.2. Two consequences.

**(a) The "alternate regime" is empty for free.** The older treatment split at $a \le 10$ versus $a \ge 11$, because the graded reduction $F = t^{21a}G$ degenerates when $v = 30-3a < 0$ and must be replaced by $F = t^{210}G'$ with a *descending* cascade. That whole apparatus is now unnecessary: Theorem 7.2 is uniform in $a$, so $\{a \ge 11\}$ is empty outright. This matters for a specific reason: the enumeration of that regime was **capped at $a + \sum b_i \le 15$**, so killing its listed branches never said anything about $a \ge 16$. A uniform bound needs no enumeration. (The cap-free $a_t \le 10$ of Corollary 11.7 already suffices for this, and is elementary — but it is a by-product, not a spine step.)

**(b) Independent corroboration.** The same collision refutes $a_t = 12$ and $a_t = 14$, re-emptying the six surviving alternate-regime branches by a mechanism completely different from the place-dichotomy route that first closed them: there, horn 1 of Theorem 11.6 is infeasible for $a \ge 11$ since $3a > 30$ (`alt_level12.py` **L6.1**, and this is where $v_t(\Phi) = 30$ being an *equality* rather than a bound is essential — **L5.0**; a mere $\ge 30$ would not exclude horn 1), and horn 2 needs $\rho = (30-a)/2 \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $0 \le \rho < a$, which is empty for odd $a$ and below the slice bound for even $a \ge 12$ (**L5.3**, **L5.6**, and the cap-free conclusion at **L6.4**). Two mechanisms, one answer — and the mandatory non-vacuity control **L5.4** confirms horn 1 *must stay feasible* for $a \le 10$, as it does.

**(c) A clean negative.** §7 cannot be pushed to kill $a_t = 9$. At $a_t=9$ the binding terms are $h_2h_5^2$ and $3h_1h_5h_6$, both at 21; level 14 advances only $h_7$, lifting one term from 21 to 22 while the minimum stays at 21; level 16 advances $h_8$, which does not appear in $B$; and $v_t(h_2)\ge3$ is *attained*, hence sharp. To kill $a_t=9$ this way one would need a **non-vanishing** statement about
$$[t^{21}]B = [t^3]h_2\cdot\gamma^2 + 3\gamma[t^{12}]h_7 + 3\eta\gamma[t^{11}]h_6,\qquad \gamma = [t^9]h_5 \ne 0,\ \eta = [t^1]h_1,$$
i.e. new information about *leading coefficients*, not about further valuations. Deeper cascade levels supply valuations only. This is the precise reason the collision stops at 9 — and it is why §8 exists.

### 7.5 The ansatz: $t^9 \mid R,S,T$, directly from the dictionary **[PROVED here, and re-verified independently for this paper]**

Everything §8 needs about $R,S,T$ now follows from what is already on the table. Earlier drafts obtained it from a resultant argument ($e\mid S$) plus a place trichotomy; **that apparatus is not needed** and has been moved off the spine to §11.3.

> **Lemma 7.4 (the valuation ledger at $t$).** On every admissible germ,
> $$v_t(d_2)\ge2,\quad v_t(d_1)\ge3,\quad v_t(e) = 9,\quad v_t(R)\ge10,\quad v_t(S)\ge11,\quad v_t(T)\ge12 .$$
> In particular $t^9 \mid R,\,S,\,T$, on both branches and in both configurations. No resultant, no $e\mid S$, no place trichotomy, no degree cap.

*Proof.* Four lines, each a $\min$ over the dictionary (7.1.1) evaluated on the profile (6.2.1).

$$v_t(d_2) \ge \min\bigl(v_t(h_2),\,2v_t(h_1)\bigr) = \min(3,2) = 2,$$
$$v_t(d_1) \ge \min\bigl(v_t(h_3),\,v_t(h_1){+}v_t(h_2),\,3v_t(h_1)\bigr) = \min(5,4,3) = 3,$$
$$v_t(R) \ge \min\bigl(v_t(h_6),\,v_t(h_1){+}v_t(h_5)\bigr) = \min(11,\,1{+}9) = 10,$$
$$v_t(S) \ge \min\bigl(v_t(h_7),\,v_t(h_1){+}v_t(h_6),\,2v_t(h_1){+}v_t(h_5)\bigr) = \min(12,\,12,\,11) = 11 .$$

For $T$, use $G_1 = 0$. Since $G_1 = \tfrac32d_1e^2 + 3d_2eR + 3eT + 3RS$, dividing by $3$ gives
$$e\,T \;=\; -\tfrac12 d_1e^2 \;-\; d_2\,e\,R \;-\; R\,S ,$$
whose three right-hand terms have $v_t \ge 3+18 = 21$, $\ge 2+9+10 = 21$, and $\ge 10+11 = 21$. Hence $v_t(eT) \ge 21$, and $v_t(e) = 9$ exactly (Theorem 7.3), so $v_t(T) \ge 12$. $\square$

Four remarks, because this is a compression and compressions are where errors hide.

* **It does not need cascade level 12.** With only the audited rows $v_t(h_{1..5}) \ge (1,3,5,7,9)$ and Lemma 6.1's consequences $v_t(h_6)\ge10$, $v_t(h_7)\ge11$, the four minima read $2,\,3,\,\min(10,10)=10,\,\min(11,11,11)=11$ — **unchanged**. Level 12, which is the zero-margin load-bearing input of §7.3, is provably inert here (`spine9_audit.py` **G4**; `sub1_spine9.py` **X1b**).
* **The $T$ estimate is a three-way tie at 21.** All three terms of $eT$ land on exactly $21$, so *every one* of the four inputs $v_t(d_1)\ge3$, $v_t(d_2)\ge2$, $v_t(R)\ge10$, $v_t(S)\ge11$ is load-bearing, and $v_t(T)\ge12$ is the sharp conclusion from them. Under the stronger ledger some repository documents record ($v_t(d_1)\ge5$, $v_t(d_2)\ge3$) the three terms land on $23$, $22$, $21$ and the conclusion is the **same** $v_t(T)\ge12$ — so the lemma does not depend on which reading of the $d$-ledger one takes, which is worth knowing because the two readings disagree.
* **The estimates cannot be improved from the profile.** Generic $h_i$ obeying (6.2.1) attain $v_t(R) = 10$, $v_t(S) = 11$, $v_t(eT) = 21$ exactly (re-verified here over 200 random profiles, in both the level-10 and the level-12 regime). So no rearrangement of the same inputs yields $t^{11}\mid R$ — and independently, $v_t(R)\ge11$ is recorded as **false** at `spine9_audit.py` **G5**. §8's ansatz deliberately uses only the weaker $t^9$, because that is what makes the reduction (8.1.1) come out with the exponents it has; the extra divisibility is spare, and §8.4 spends part of it on the $z$-window.
* **What it consumes:** (A1) through $G_1$ only, (A2)+(A3) through the profile and the dictionary, and Theorem 7.3. It is cap-free and branch-free.

## 8. The five cases: $\deg\Pi \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$

Fix an admissible germ. By Theorems 3.4 and 7.3,
$$e = \gamma\,t^9\,\Pi,\qquad \Pi \mid q\ \text{squarefree},\quad k := \deg\Pi \in\{0,1,2,3,4\},\quad q = \Pi\,Q_\Pi,\quad \Phi = c\,t^{30}\,\Pi\,Q_\Pi,\ \ c = -\tfrac1{6630}.$$
By Lemma 7.4, $t^9 \mid R,S,T$; write
$$R = t^9A,\qquad S = t^9B,\qquad T = t^9C .$$
We work with the configuration-(1) caps of Lemma 2.5 (they are the looser pair; see §9 for configuration (2)), giving $\deg A \le 9$, $\deg u \le 6$ where $u := \gamma d_2$, etc.

### 8.1 The reduction **[AUDITED: `sub1_spine9.py` P3–P4, 37/37; `spine9_audit.py` B1–B5; verified independently here]**

Substituting the ansatz into $G_1,G_2,G_3$ and $K$ and dividing **without supplying a quotient**:
$$G_1 = 3t^{18}g_1,\qquad G_2 = \tfrac32t^{18}g_2,\qquad G_3 = 3t^{18}g_3,\qquad K = -\gamma\,t^{27}\,\Pi\cdot\square,$$
where, with $\mu := 2c/\gamma$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_1 &= \tfrac12\gamma^2d_1\Pi^2 + \gamma\Pi(d_2A + C) + AB,\\
g_2 &= d_2A^2 + 2AC + B^2 - \gamma^2d_0\Pi^2,\\
g_3 &= -\gamma d_0\Pi A - \tfrac12 d_1A^2 + BC - \tfrac16\gamma^3t^9\Pi^3,\\
\square &= 3A^2 + \gamma^2 d_2\Pi^2 + 3\gamma\Pi B - \mu\,t^3\,Q_\Pi .
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1.1}
$$
Residual exactly $0$. *(Reproduced independently for this paper.)* The division is falsifiable: dividing $K$ by $t^{28}\Pi$ instead of $t^{27}\Pi$ does **not** yield a polynomial.

**One structural remark.** The boxed row carries $t^{30-3a} = t^3$ for *every* $k$. In configuration (2), where $\deg e = 10$ forces $a + k = 10$, the two exponents are coupled and a degree coincidence occurs; here $a$ is pinned and $k$ is free, so they decouple. This is why configuration (2)'s degree bookkeeping does not transfer, and also why the marked-support test below becomes *stronger* rather than weaker.

### 8.2 The marked roots **[PROVED, hand-checkable]**

Let $r$ be a root of $\Pi$ (so $k \ge 1$). Then $\square = 0$ at $y=r$ reads
$$3A(r)^2 = \mu\,(r+1)^3\,Q_\Pi(r),$$
a product of three provably nonzero factors: $\mu = 2c/\gamma \ne 0$; $(r+1) \ne 0$ because $q(-1) = 3315 \ne 0$; and $Q_\Pi(r) \ne 0$ because $q$ is squarefree. Hence **$A(r) \ne 0$**. Then $g_1 = 0$ at $y = r$ reads $A(r)B(r) = 0$, so $\Pi \mid B$ ($\Pi$ squarefree); write $B = \Pi v$.

### 8.3 The cofactor identity **[AUDITED: `sub1_spine9.py` P7; `spine9_audit.py` C3–C7; verified independently here]**

$g_1$ is linear in $C$ with leading coefficient $\gamma\Pi \ne 0$, so $C$ is **determined**:
$$C = -\frac{A(u+v) + w}{\gamma},\qquad u := \gamma d_2,\quad w := \tfrac12\gamma^2 d_1\Pi .$$
Let $\widehat g_2,\widehat g_3$ be the rows after these substitutions, and set
$$F := A(u+2v) + w,\qquad Z := A^2 - \gamma\Pi^2 v .$$

> **Theorem 8.1 (cofactor identity).** In $\mathbb{Q}[\gamma,t,A,v,u,d_1,\Pi]$,
> $$F\,Z - \tfrac16\gamma^5t^9\Pi^4 \;=\; -\gamma A\,\widehat g_2 + \gamma^2\Pi\,\widehat g_3 ,$$
> residual exactly $0$, with $d_0$ absent. Hence on every admissible germ
> $$F\,Z = \tfrac16\,\gamma^5\,t^9\,\Pi^4 . \tag{$*$}$$

*(Verified independently for this paper: residual $0$, $d_0 \notin$ the identity.)*

Three facts guard against a degree coincidence being smuggled in. Both sides have free symbols inside $\{\gamma,t,\Pi,A,v,d_0,d_1,d_2\}$ — it is an identity in a polynomial ring in those indeterminates, so **no degree, no cap and no slack count can enter a statement of that form**. The identity survives replacing $t^9$ by a free symbol $t^a$: it is the generic $a$-family identity. And $d_0$ occurs in $\widehat g_2$ and $\widehat g_3$ separately and cancels exactly, which is a coefficient identity, not a degree count.

**Where Theorem 8.1 comes from.** It is not a lucky find downstream of $a_t = 9$; it is Theorem 3.5 in the normalised coordinates of §8.1, and it would have been there for any value of $a$. Substituting the ansatz $e = \gamma t^9\Pi$, $R = t^9A$, $S = t^9\Pi v$, $T = t^9C$ into the two Hankel minors of §3.5 gives, after eliminating $C$ by $g_1 = 0$,
$$W \;=\; -\,t^{18}\,Z,\qquad\qquad \mathcal Z \;=\; -\,t^{18}\,\Pi\,F$$
(writing $\mathcal Z := eT - RS$ for §3.5's second minor, to keep it apart from §8's $Z = A^2-\gamma\Pi^2v$). Hence
$$6\,W\,\mathcal Z \;=\; 6\,t^{36}\,\Pi\,F\,Z \qquad\text{and}\qquad e^5 = \gamma^5t^{45}\Pi^5,$$
so the universal $6W\mathcal Z = e^5$ reads $6\,t^{36}\Pi\,FZ = \gamma^5t^{45}\Pi^5$, which after cancelling $6t^{36}\Pi$ is **exactly** $(*)$. The whole content of §8.3 is therefore the normalisation, not the identity. *(Verified symbolically, both minors and the collapse, with sign and factor mutations: `toric_syzygy.py` C2–C4.)*

Since the right side of $(*)$ is nonzero, $F \ne 0$, $Z \ne 0$, and
$$\deg F + \deg Z = 9 + 4k\quad\text{exactly, for every }k. \tag{$*$deg}$$

### 8.4 $Z$ is a monomial **[PROVED, hand-checkable]**

By §8.2, $Z(r) = A(r)^2 \ne 0$ at every root of $\Pi$, so $\gcd(Z,\Pi) = 1$ (and at $k=0$ this is trivial). In $(*)$, $\tfrac16$ and $\gamma^5$ are **units of the coefficient field** — $\gamma$ is the leading coefficient of $e$, a nonzero *scalar*, not a polynomial — and $t$ is prime in the UFD $\overline K[y]$ with $\gcd(t,\Pi) = 1$. Nothing else hides in the right side. Hence
$$Z = \zeta\,t^{z},\qquad \zeta \ne 0,\qquad 0 \le z \le 9 . \tag{8.4.1}$$

**The window $2 \le z \le 6$.** From $(*)$, $v_t(F) + v_t(Z) = 9$. Lemma 7.4 supplies the whole ledger. Since $v_t(\Pi) = 0$ ($\Pi \mid q$ and $q(-1)\ne0$) and $\gamma$ is a nonzero scalar,
$$v_t(A) = v_t(R)-9 \ge 1,\qquad v_t(v) = v_t(S)-9 \ge 2,\qquad v_t(u) = v_t(d_2) \ge 2,\qquad v_t(w) = v_t(d_1) \ge 3,$$
and therefore
$$v_t(Z) \ge \min\bigl(2v_t(A),\,v_t(v)\bigr) \ge \min(2,2) = 2,\qquad v_t(F)\ge\min\bigl(v_t(A)+\min(v_t(u),v_t(v)),\,v_t(w)\bigr)\ge\min(3,3) = 3,$$
so $z = v_t(Z) \in [2,\,9-3] = [2,6]$. **Cascade level 12 is inert here**: by the first remark after Lemma 7.4 the ledger is unchanged if the cascade is truncated at level 10, so the same input that is zero-margin load-bearing in §7.3 buys nothing in §8 (`spine9_audit.py` **G4**, `sub1_spine9.py` **X1b**). Nor can the window be tightened by claiming $v_t(R)\ge11$: that is **false** (`spine9_audit.py` **G5**).

Note the important asymmetry: **$z \le 9$ is free** (it costs no cap and no cascade), and the three cases $k=1,2,3$ below die for *every* $z$ in $[0,9]$, hence consume no valuation input whatsoever. Only $k=0$ needs $z \le 6$.

**Why the exponent is $\Pi^4$.** The $4$ is a forced local contact order, not an artifact of the $t^9$ normalisation, and §3.5 says so directly. On a $G$-point with $e\ne0$, Lemma 11.5 gives $S = e\bar S$, so §3.5's second minor factors as $\mathcal Z = e\,\mathcal Z_1$ with $\mathcal Z_1 = T - R\bar S$, and (3.5.1) becomes the divisor law (3.5.2)–(3.5.3):
$$6\,W\,\mathcal Z_1 = e^4,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad v_\beta(W) + v_\beta(\mathcal Z_1) = 4\,v_\beta(e)\ \text{ at every place }\beta .$$
Now take $\beta$ a root of $\Pi$. Three facts, each already proved:

* $v_\beta(e) = 1$, because $e = \gamma t^9\Pi$ with $\Pi \mid q$ **squarefree** and $t(\beta)\ne0$ (as $q(-1) = 3315 \ne 0$);
* $v_\beta(W) = 0$, because $e(\beta) = 0$ makes $W(\beta) = e(\beta)S(\beta) - R(\beta)^2 = -R(\beta)^2$, and $R(\beta)\ne0$ is §8.2's marked-root fact;
* therefore $v_\beta(\mathcal Z_1) = 4\cdot1 - 0 = 4$.

Since $\mathcal Z_1 = -t^9F/\gamma$ in the normalised coordinates, this is $v_\beta(F) = 4$ at every root of $\Pi$, i.e. $\Pi^4 \mid F$ — which, given $\gcd(Z,\Pi) = 1$ from $Z(\beta) = A(\beta)^2\ne0$, is precisely how the $\Pi^4$ of $(*)$ distributes: **all of it sits in $F$, none of it in $Z$.** The exponent $4$ traces back to the $e^5$ of Theorem 3.5, shed of one factor of $e$ by $e\mid S$; it does not know about $a_t$, which is why $(*)$ survives replacing $t^9$ by $t^a$. A non-squarefree $\Pi$ would force contact order $8$, and $W(\beta) = 0$ would force $3$ — so the value is sensitive to exactly the two inputs named. *(`toric_syzygy.py` D1–D6: the divisor law, the two marked-root facts, and an exhaustive check that $v_\beta(W) = 0$ is load-bearing — $120$ of the $150$ factorisations violate the conclusion once it is dropped.)*

### 8.5 The marked-support test — formerly the one irreducible machine step, now hand-checkable

Substituting $A^2 = Z + \gamma\Pi^2v$ and $B = \Pi v$ into $\square = 0$:
$$3A^2 + \gamma\Pi^2(u+3v) = \mu t^3Q_\Pi,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \gamma\Pi^2(u+6v) = \mu t^3 Q_\Pi - 3\zeta t^{z} .$$
Since $t$ is a unit at every root of $\Pi$:

$$\boxed{\;\Pi^2 \;\Big|\; \bigl(\mu\,t^3\,Q_\Pi - 3\zeta\,t^{z}\bigr),\qquad \mu \ne 0,\ \zeta \ne 0 .\;} \tag{8.5.1}$$

The condition is homogeneous in $(\mu,\zeta)$, so normalise $\mu = 1$. **$\Pi$ need not be rational** — $q$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with Galois group $S_4$, so a genuine degree-1, 2 or 3 factor lives in an extension. Two structurally different decision procedures were run and required to agree on all $40$ pairs $(k,z)$, $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, $z\in[0,9]$; they do.

* **Route 1 (ideal).** Pose $\Pi$ monic of degree $k$ with unknown coefficients, $Q_\Pi$ of degree $4-k$, subject to $q - \Pi Q_\Pi = 0$ (5 equations) and $\mathrm{rem}(t^3Q_\Pi - 3\zeta t^z,\Pi^2) = 0$ ($2k$ equations), saturated at $\zeta \ne 0$. A **unit ideal** means no such factorisation exists in *any* field extension.
* **Route 2 (rank).** For fixed $\Pi$, (8.5.1) is **linear** in $(\mu,\zeta)$. Let $M$ be the $2k\times2$ matrix with columns $\mathrm{rem}(t^3Q_\Pi,\Pi^2)$ and $\mathrm{rem}(-3t^z,\Pi^2)$. Both columns are nonzero — $\gcd(\Pi,t)=1$ and $\deg\Pi^2>0$ give the second; $q$ squarefree gives $\gcd(\Pi,Q_\Pi)=1$ so $\Pi^2 \nmid Q_\Pi$, giving the first — and **both are asserted at runtime**, so the criterion cannot be silently vacuous. A solution with $\mu\ne0$ *and* $\zeta\ne0$ exists **iff $\mathrm{rank}\,M = 1$**, i.e. iff every $2\times2$ minor vanishes. No saturation and no normalisation needed. $\Pi$ is realised exactly: $k=4$ over $\mathbb{Q}$; $k=1,3$ over $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(q)$; $k=2$ over $\mathbb{Q}[p]/(\text{sextic})$ where $32768p^6+24576p^5+16384p^4+5632p^3-10080p^2-2680p-495$ is the resolvent for $p = -(r_i+r_j)$ and is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, so its single Galois orbit covers all six 2-subsets.

> **Theorem 8.2.** Over $[0,9]$, condition (8.5.1) is **infeasible for $k = 1, 2, 3$ and every $z$**, and for $k=4$ is feasible only at $z = 3$.

This is step #8 of §13's list — the one step of the proof that is not hand-checkable in principle. It is genuine Gröbner / number-field work.

**Theorem 8.2 was independently replicated while writing this paper**, by three constructions sharing no code with either route above, and with a non-vacuity control (see §13.3): $k=1$ by the hand gcd route below, over $\mathbb{Q}$; $k=2$ by posing $q/2048 = (y^2+Ay+B)(y^2+Cy+D)$ and checking that the ideal generated by those four equations together with all six $2\times2$ minors is the **unit ideal over $\mathbb{Q}$** for every $z\in[0,9]$ — while the four factorisation equations *alone* are not, so the test is not vacuous; $k=3$ by the rank test with coefficients reduced in $\mathbb{Q}[r]/(q)$; $k=4$ by the rank test over $\mathbb{Q}$, which returns exactly $z=3$. This is now a three-party result.

Two partial hand routes exist and both are worth having:

* **$k=1$, fully by hand.** With $q = (y-r)Q_\Pi$, the $2\times2$ determinant is $3(r+1)^{z+2}\bigl((3-z)Q_\Pi(r) + (r+1)Q_\Pi'(r)\bigr)$, i.e. the condition reduces to $q(r) = 0$ together with $(r+1)q''(r) = 2(z-3)q'(r)$, that is to
$$\gcd\bigl(q,\ (y+1)q'' - 2(z-3)q'\bigr) = 1 ,$$
and that gcd is $1$ for every $z \in [0,9]$. (A third implementation confirms this.)
* **$k=3$, structurally.** $\deg(t^3Q_\Pi) = 4$, $\deg t^z \le 9$, $\deg\Pi^2 = 6$. For $z\le5$ the numerator has degree $<6$, so it must vanish identically — impossible since $Q_\Pi$ is linear and $\mu\ne0$. For $z=6$ the leading term forces the cofactor and evaluating at $y=-1$ gives $-3\zeta\Pi(-1)^2 = 0$ with $\Pi(-1)\ne0$.

**Non-vacuity, graded honestly.** The test returns FEASIBLE at $(k,z)=(4,3)$ on the genuine $q$ — which is precisely why $k=4$ needs §8.6. The $k=1$ machinery returns FEASIBLE at exactly $z=3$ on the synthetic squarefree quartic $y^4+y^3-9y^2+7$ (root $r=1$ with $q''(1)=0$, $q(-1) = -2 \ne 0$). The $k=2$ machinery returns FEASIBLE on a synthetic squarefree quartic with $q(-1)\ne0$. **For $k=3$ no positive control is possible**: with $\deg Q_\Pi = 1$, $\deg\Pi^2 = 6$, $\gcd(\Pi,t)=1$, *no* $z$ can work for *any* squarefree quartic with $q(-1)\ne0$. So the $k=3$ kill is structural rather than arithmetic — a *stronger* kill, but one that cannot be distinguished from a bug by a positive control. It is cross-checked by two independent implementations and by the hand count above.

> **Corollary 8.3.** No admissible germ has $\deg\Pi \in \{1,2,3\}$.

These three kills consume **no valuation input, no cascade, no level-12 result, and no degree cap.** They rest on $a_t = 9$, on $e\mid\Phi$, and on the arithmetic of this specific quartic.

### 8.6 The degree ledger — cases $k=4$ and $k=0$ **[AUDITED: `sub1_spine9.py` P13–P15; `spine9_audit.py` F1–F14]**

$(*\text{deg})$ fixes $\deg F = 9+4k-z$. Lemma 2.5, configuration (1), gives $\deg F \le \max(\deg A + \max(\deg u,\deg v),\,\deg w)$:

| $k$ | $\deg A$ | $\deg u$ | $\deg v$ | $\deg w$ | cap on $\deg F$ | $\deg F = 9+4k-z$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 9 | 6 | 12 | 9 | 21 | $9-z$ |
| 1 | 9 | 6 | 11 | 10 | 20 | $13-z$ |
| 2 | 9 | 6 | 10 | 11 | 19 | $17-z$ |
| 3 | 9 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 18 | $21-z$ |
| 4 | 9 | 6 | 8 | 13 | **17** | $25-z$ |

> **Corollary 8.4 ($k = 4$).** Theorem 8.2 pins $z = 3$, so $\deg F = 22 > 17$. **Contradiction**, with no cascade input. A second, independent route: even ignoring Theorem 8.2, $z\le6$ forces $\deg F \ge 19 > 17$.

The ledger is **not** a blanket argument: for each of $k=0,1,2,3$ it permits some admissible $z$. And $k=4$'s kill has a wide margin — the $\deg R$ cap would have to reach 23 to switch it off.

> **Corollary 8.5 ($k = 0$).** Here $\Pi = 1$, $Q_\Pi = q$, and §8.5 is vacuous. Eliminating $v$ from $Z = A^2 - \gamma v$ in the boxed row gives, with residual $0$,
> $$\gamma u = \mu\,t^3q - 6A^2 + 3\zeta\,t^{z},\qquad \deg u \le 6 .$$
> Now $\deg(\mu t^3q) = 7$ exactly ($2048\mu \ne 0$). If $\deg A \ge 4$, then $-6A^2$ has **even** degree $\ge 8$, above both $7$ and $z \le 6$: an uncancellable leading term, so $\deg(\mathrm{RHS}) \ge 8 > 6$. If $\deg A \le 3$ (including $A = 0$), then $\deg(6A^2)\le6$ and $\deg(3\zeta t^z)\le6$, so the degree-7 term of $\mu t^3q$ survives and $\deg(\mathrm{RHS}) = 7 > 6$. No $\deg A$ gives $2\deg A = 7$. **Contradiction.**

This is the *only* case that consumes the slice cascade, and it consumes it tightly. Three independent zero-margin dependencies, each verified to switch the kill off when weakened by exactly one: $\deg d_2 \le 7$ instead of $6$ — kill off; $z \le 7$ instead of $6$ — kill off (at $z=7$ the term $3\zeta t^7$ can cancel the leading term of $2048\mu t^3q$); and $v_t(d_1)\ge3$, which is what makes $v_t(F)\ge3$ and comes from $3v_t(h_1)$, so $v_t(h_1)\ge1$ is load-bearing too. The dichotomy was also re-posed as an explicit leading-term search over $\deg A \in \{A=0\}\cup[0,39]$ and $z\in[0,6]$, registering a contradiction **only** when a *unique* contributor attains the maximal degree, so that no cancellation could rescue it.

### 8.7 Configuration (1) is empty

> **Theorem 8.6.** There is no admissible germ in configuration (1).

Theorem 7.3 gives $a_t = 9$; Corollary 8.3 kills $k=1,2,3$; Corollaries 8.4 and 8.5 kill $k=4$ and $k=0$; Theorem 3.4 says $k \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$; and Lemma 3.3 disposes of $e\equiv0$. $\square$

## 9. Configuration (2)

Configuration (2) is empty by three routes, and we record all three because they are genuinely different.

**(a) A fortiori from §8.** Configuration (2)'s caps are *tighter* in every entry that §8 consumes ($d_2 \le 4$ against 6; $R\le12$ against 18; $S\le14$ against 21). Since §8's kills are degree *contradictions*, tighter caps make them easier, and §8.5's three kills are cap-free. ⚠️ **This route is our observation and was not machine-checked**: the checkers behind §8 were run only at the configuration-(1) caps. We state it as a remark, not as evidence.

**(b) The degree forcing plus §8.** Here `[QQ1]`-independent bookkeeping gives more. With the configuration-(2) caps of Lemma 2.5 — $\deg d_2\le4$, $\deg R\le12$, $\deg S\le14$, $\deg e\le10$ — the right side of (3.2.1) has degree $E + \max(4+2E,\,E+14,\,24)$ where $E := \deg e$. Against $\deg\Phi = 34$:

| $E$ | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | **10** | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 11.1 A Catalan law for a Belyi family **[PROVED here; count independently CHECKED to $k=19$]**

The context is the *top band* of configuration (1). In the band coordinates $t_{\mathrm{band}} = xy^2$, $z = y^{-1}$, the top-band coefficient polynomials $a$ (degree 7) and $d$ (degree 10) satisfy the single top-layer equation
$$2a\,d' - 3a'\,d = t_{\mathrm{band}}^{\,2},\tag{J4}$$
and $\beta := t_{\mathrm{band}}\,d^2/a^3$ is a Belyi map of degree $21$ with passport
$$\bigl(2^{10},1 \;\big|\; 3^{7} \;\big|\; 17,1^{4}\bigr).$$
The passport is **forced** by (J4) — $a$ has 7 distinct nonzero roots, $d$ has 10, $\gcd(a,d)=1$, all proved — so the correspondence between (J4)-solutions and dessins is a bijection, not a generic-fibre statement, and Riemann–Hurwitz is an *equality* ($10+14+16 = 40 = 2\cdot21-2$), which forces the third fibre and forbids a fourth branch point. In the family indexed by odd $k$ the passport is
$$\Bigl(2^{(3k-1)/2},\,1 \;\Big|\; 3^{k} \;\Big|\; \tfrac{5k-1}{2},\,1^{(k+1)/2}\Bigr),\qquad \deg = 3k,$$
and $(72,108)$ is $k = 7$. (For even $k$ both $(3k-1)/2$ and $(5k-1)/2$ are non-integral, so the family has no even members.)

> **Theorem 11.1.** For odd $k$ put $m = (k-1)/2$. The genus-0 dessins with the above passport are in canonical bijection with **rooted plane binary trees on $m$ internal nodes**. Hence there are exactly $C_m$ of them; every one is connected; every one has trivial automorphism group.

*Proof.* Let $n = 3k = 6m+3$. Read the triple as a map: $\sigma_2$ (type $3^k$) gives $k$ trivalent vertices; $\sigma_1$ (type $2^{(3k-1)/2}\,1$) gives $(3k-1)/2$ edges and **one leg**, its unique fixed point; faces are the cycles of $\sigma_3$.

*Faces of degree 1 are self-loops.* $\sigma_1\sigma_2(d) = d$ means $\sigma_2(d) = \sigma_1(d)$, i.e. $d$ and $\sigma_2(d)$ are joined by an edge — a loop at a trivalent vertex whose two darts are consecutive. The passport prescribes $(k+1)/2 = m+1$ monogons and one big face.

*Delete the monogons.* Turn the leg into an edge to a phantom univalent vertex: $V = 2m+2$, $E = 3m+2$, $F = m+2$, and Euler gives $2$, so genus 0. Deleting the $m+1$ loop edges cannot disconnect and leaves $V = 2m+2$, $E = 2m+1$, hence $F=1$ and $V-E = 1$: a **plane tree**, with $m$ vertices of degree 3 and $m+2$ of degree 1, since $(m+1)\cdot1 + m\cdot3 + 1\cdot1 = 4m+2 = 2E$.

*Root it.* The leg is canonical, so the tree is rooted at a leaf; rooted plane trees with $m$ degree-3 vertices and $m+2$ leaves are exactly rooted plane binary trees on $m$ internal nodes — $C_m$ of them.

*Invert.* Attach a loop at each non-root leaf and reopen the root edge into a leg. The two cyclic orders $(a,b,c)$ and $(a,c,b)$ at such a leaf are conjugate by the transposition of the two loop darts, which is a dessin isomorphism, so the inverse is well defined; the third partition's shape is then forced: $3k - (m+1) = (5k-1)/2$. $\square$

> **Corollary 11.2 (transitivity, every $k$).** An orbit not containing the long cycle consists of $\sigma_3$-fixed points, so $\sigma_1\sigma_2 = \mathrm{id}$ there, so $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1^{-1}$; an element of order dividing both 2 and 3 is trivial, but $\sigma_2$ has no fixed points. Hence the orbit is empty.
>
> **Corollary 11.3 ($|\mathrm{Aut}| = 1$, every $k$).** The centraliser of a transitive group acts semiregularly; any automorphism permutes $\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_1)$, a single point, hence fixes it, hence is trivial.

**Corollary 11.3 repairs a load-bearing gap, not a cosmetic one.** The Hurwitz number was being computed as $N/n!$, which is the dessin count *only if* $|\mathrm{Aut}| = 1$; the previous rigidity argument bounded $|\mathrm{Aut}|$ only by $\gcd(3k,(5k-1)/2)$, which is **3, not 1, at $k = 5,11,17,23,\dots$** So at $k=5$ and $k=11$ — two of the data points the law was inferred from — there was no proof that the number computed was the number of dessins. Corollary 11.2 likewise replaces an exhaustive $S_3$ search done only at $k=7$.

Independent confirmation without trees: Frobenius character sums give $1,1,2,5,14,42,132,429,1430,4862 = C_0,\dots,C_9$ for $k = 1,\dots,19$, calibrated at $k=1$ and negative-controlled (perturbing the third partition at $k=7$ to $(16,2,1,1,1)$ gives 15, not 5). The bijection itself is exact for $m \le 7$ (all 429 dessins), against a canonicaliser verified sound over 788 random relabellings and verified discriminating.

**Honest caveat on novelty.** This is monogon deletion on a genus-0 trivalent constellation — a routine technique in the dessins literature — and "Catalan numbers count rooted plane binary trees" is classical. It is a clean and complete result, but **almost certainly folklore-adjacent, and we would not present it as a contribution to the Hurwitz-number literature.** Its value here is threefold: it upgrades an inferred law to proved, it closes the $|\mathrm{Aut}|$ gap, and it makes the following decomposition intelligible rather than coincidental.

**The payoff: Helali's degree-35 block.** The solution variety of (J4) in the 19 unknowns $a_1..a_8,d_2..d_{12}$ carries a 2-dimensional group $(a,d)\mapsto(\alpha\,a(\lambda t),\alpha^{-1}d(\lambda t))$; the $t^{17}$ coefficient equation is identically zero, leaving 17 equations, so the variety is 2-dimensional and the group acts with finite stabiliser. Helali's normalising slice $a_1 = a_8 = 1$ meets each orbit in exactly **7** points: preserving (J4) needs $\mu\nu\lambda^3 = 1$, and preserving the slice forces $\mu\lambda = 1$ and $\mu\lambda^8 = 1$, whose ratio is $\lambda^7 = 1$, with $a_k \mapsto \lambda^{k-1}a_k$, i.e. weights $(1,\dots,6)$ on $a_2..a_7$ — none $\equiv 0 \bmod 7$, so a nontrivial fixed point forces $a_2=\dots=a_7=0$, which is not a solution. **The action is free.** Therefore
$$\mathrm{vdim}(\text{first block}) = 7\cdot\#\text{orbits} = 7\cdot\#\text{dessins} = 7\times5 = 35 .$$
The $\mu_7$ grading is visible in his own lex basis, where $H(a_7)$ is literally a polynomial in $a_7^7$. And the degree-5 field $L = \mathbb{Q}[w]/(w^5-w^4+3w^3+3w^2+26)$ that his endgame descends to is the field of moduli of a **single** Galois orbit of the 5 dessins, with Galois group $S_5$: $[L:\mathbb{Q}] = 5$ *is* the Hurwitz number. A character sum would have named that field in seconds, before any CAS ran.

Two limits, stated plainly. **This governs only the top band** — (J4) is one layer, and the negative bands, the $s = \pm c$ split, the $r$-elimination, the $h=0/h\ne0$ branching and the large hard identity get nothing from it. And the separate ladder $\mathrm{vdim} = k\cdot\mathrm{Hurwitz}$ **remains inferred on two nontrivial data points, not four**: $k=1$ is trivial (an empty variable list), $k=3\to3$ and $k=5\to10$ are the only independent measurements, and an attempt at $k=9$ (predicted 126) had not returned after ~50 minutes. A timeout is never a verdict.

Finally, a warning: "Riemann–Hurwitz balances" is an **identity for this family, not a test** — it holds for every $(m,n,k,\ell)$ — and any use of it must be accompanied by a discriminating control.

### 11.2 The divisor-syzygy criterion: $q_{\mathrm{window}} \mid w(e)$, not $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 1$ **[PROVED; exhaustive over 1 529 566 splits]**

Why does the K-syzygy exist at $(72,108)$ and not elsewhere? The recorded explanation was $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 1$, where $q_{\mathrm{window}}$ is the denominator of $\mathrm{ord}_y(\Phi)/M$ in lowest terms ($M := w(\Phi)$ the $u$-slice weight of $\Phi$; $w(d_m) = t-m$, so $w(e) = t+1$). At $(72,108)$: $\mathrm{ord}_y\Phi/M = 204/17 = 12$, so $\alpha = 12$, $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 1$. At $(75,125)$: $80/29$, so $\alpha = 80$, $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 29$, and the $y$-order cap $L(w) = \lceil \alpha w/q\rceil$ is superadditive off the period:
$$L(5) + L(24) = 14 + 67 = 81 \;>\; 80 = L(29) = \mathrm{ord}_y\Phi ,$$
so no relation $c\Phi = eB$ can exist there.

**Correction (2026-07-26).** An earlier draft gave $(75,125)$ as $201/36 = 67/12$ with $\alpha = 67$, $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 12$, $M = 36$, $w(e) = 6$ — the superseded $(5,20)$ chart data repaired in commit `2adb92a`. The corrected reading is **strictly stronger**. Because $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 29 = M$ *exactly*, no split with $1 \le w < M$ is a multiple of $q_{\mathrm{window}}$, so the carry is $1$ on **every** admissible split and there is no weight at which the obstruction could vanish; at $67/12$ against $M = 36$ it would have vanished at $w \in \{12,24\}$. So $(75,125)$ is *more* firmly obstructed by this mechanism than the earlier numbers claimed, not less. Pinned in `minimal_core.py` F0/F3 (60/60), which carried the same superseded constants until 2026-07-26.

> **Theorem 11.4 (criterion).** Let $\alpha/q$ be $\mathrm{ord}_y(\Phi)/M$ in lowest terms, so $\gcd(\alpha,q) = 1$ and $q \mid M$; let $L(w) = \lceil\alpha w/q\rceil$ and $\mathrm{carry}(w_1,w_2) := L(w_1)+L(w_2)-L(w_1+w_2)$. For any candidate relation $c\Phi = eB$ with $c$ a nonzero constant and $w(e)+w(B) = M$, $1 \le w(e) < M$:
> $$\mathrm{carry}\bigl(w(e),\,M-w(e)\bigr) = 0 \iff q_{\mathrm{window}} \mid w(e),$$
> and the carry is exactly $0$ or $1$, never more.

*Proof.* Write $\alpha w(e)/q = A + r/q$; the carry is the $\lceil\cdot\rceil$ defect, so it is $0$ iff $r = 0$, and $r = 0 \iff q \mid \alpha w(e) \iff q \mid w(e)$ since $\gcd(\alpha,q)=1$. $\square$

Verified exhaustively over **1 529 566** admissible splits with **zero mismatches**, and mutation-controlled: **30 326** admissible splits have carry $0$ with $q_{\mathrm{window}} \ne 1$. So $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 1$ is **sufficient but not necessary** — it is merely the special case that divides every weight.

Three consequences, one of which is a refutation.

* The correct statement is "$(72,108)$ admits the mechanism because $q_{\mathrm{window}} \mid w(e)$". Other corners can support it; the search space is larger than was believed.
* **Sporadicity is irrelevant.** $q_{\mathrm{window}}\mid w(e)$ is an arithmetic condition on one corner's $(\mathrm{ord}_y\Phi, M, w(e))$; nothing connects it to membership in a GGV5 family. $(72,108)$ *is* sporadic — it appears in no family, only in the length-1 chain $(8,28)\,|\,(11/4,7)\,|\,(3,2)\,|\,108$ and a length-2 chain — but that is a separate fact and the two are logically unrelated. **This refutes the framing of the syzygy as a feature of sporadic corners.**
* The $F_2$ family law is $q_{\mathrm{window}} = 12a-7 \in \{17,29,41,\dots\}$ (`f2_tower.py`, corrected chart; $17$ at $a=2$, $29$ at $a=3$, both prime). It still rules out that whole family, and the reason is *stronger* than a coincidence: every period in the family already exceeds $w(e) = t+1 = 5$, so $w(e)$ cannot be a nonzero multiple of any of them. (The value $6$ carried by an earlier draft of this bullet is the pre-repair $t = 5$ reading; post-repair both $(50,75)$ and $(75,125)$ have $t = 4$, and `toric_general.py` already used $w(e) = 5$.) **Correction (2026-07-26):** an earlier draft stated this law as $5a-3 \in \{7,12,17,\dots\}$, computed off the superseded $(5,20)$ chart data repaired in commit `2adb92a`; see `PASSPORT_75_125_REPAIR.md` §R1. ### 11.3 Divisor consequences: $e \mid S$ and the place trichotomy **[EXACT-CHECKED: `divisor_consequences.py` A1–A7, 29/29; `t1_branch.py` `C3_e_divides_S`, `C11_place_theorems`, 15/15; `alt_rebuild.py` `B3_e_divides_S`, `B4_trichotomy`, 18/18]**

These two results were the spine of §4 in an earlier draft, where they were used to obtain $t^9 \mid R,S,T$. **They are no longer on the spine.** Lemma 7.4 gets the same conclusion — indeed the sharper $t^{10}\mid R$, $t^{11}\mid S$, $t^{12}\mid T$ — from the shift dictionary and $G_1$ alone. They are kept because both are real, cheaply verified, and hold under the *weakest* hypothesis in the paper: they are statements about a bare $G$-point.

> **Lemma 11.5 ($e \mid S$).** On every $G$-point with $e \not\equiv 0$, $S/e$ is a polynomial.

Three independent proofs exist. The shortest: $G_2$ and $G_3$ are linear in $T$ with $T$-coefficients $3R$ and $3S$, and $G_1 = 3e(T - T_f)$ with $T_f = -R(S/e + d_2) - d_1e/2$. Hence
$$A_2 := \tfrac23(eG_2 - RG_1),\qquad A_3 := -\tfrac13(eG_3 - SG_1)$$
are both polynomial, both $T$-free, both in $(G_1,G_2,G_3)$. Eliminating $R$ by the Sylvester resultant, **with the cofactors read off the adjugate so that ideal membership is a certificate rather than a citation**, gives
$$\mathrm{Res}_R(A_2,A_3) = -2e\Bigl[S^7 + \sum_{i=1}^{7} e^i\alpha_i S^{7-i}\Bigr]$$
with every $\alpha_i$ a genuine polynomial in $d_0,d_1,d_2,e$ (verified by exact division). $S/e$ is thus integral over $\mathbb{Q}[y]$, which is integrally closed, so $S/e \in \mathbb{Q}[y]$. No colon ideal is taken anywhere, no valuation and no case analysis. $\square$

> **Theorem 11.6 (place trichotomy).** On every $G$-point with $e\not\equiv0$, at **every** place $\beta$,
> $$v_\beta(R) \ge v_\beta(e) \qquad\text{or}\qquad v_\beta(\Phi) = v_\beta(e) + 2v_\beta(R).$$

*Proof.* In (3.2.1) the three right-hand terms have $v_\beta \ge 3m$, $= 2m + v_\beta(S) \ge 3m$ (Lemma 11.5), and $= m + 2\rho$, where $m := v_\beta(e)$, $\rho := v_\beta(R)$. If $\rho < m$ then $m+2\rho \le 3m-2 < 3m$, so the $R$-term is the **strict unique** minimum and $v_\beta(2\Phi)$ equals it. $\square$

(The name is historical; as `alt_level12.py` **L5.1** records, the statement is a *dichotomy*. The "trichotomy" that is genuinely three-way is the unrelated terminal branch split $T_1\mid T_2\mid T_3$.)

> **Corollary 11.7 (a cap-free $a_t \le 10$).** At $\beta = -1$: either $t^{a}\mid R$, or $a + 2v_t(R) = 30$ with $v_t(R)\le a-1$, which forces $a\ge11$ **and** $a$ even. Hence $t^a\mid R$ whenever $a\le10$ or $a$ is odd; and combined with Lemma 11.5 and $T = -R(S/e+d_2)-d_1e/2$,
> $$t^a \mid R,\,S,\,T\qquad\text{for } a \le 10,\ \text{on both branches.}$$
> Moreover horn 1 gives immediately $a_t \le 10$: if $v_t(R)\ge a$ then every term of $B$ has $v_t\ge2a$, so $30 = a+v_t(B)\ge3a$. With the parity/integrality of horn 2 this is $a_t \le 10$ with **no degree cap, no stratum premise, and no slice family** — the only bound in the paper that needs none of (A2), (A3), (A4).

### 12.2 `[QQ1]`, and where it is spent

The statement and its proof are **Proposition 2.1**; this subsection is about its standing, not its content. ## 13. Machine verification: what is checked, by what, at what grade

### 13.1 The grading ladder

Two distinct schemes run in this repository and must not be merged.

**Evidence levels (DAG nodes and edges):** `open < claimed < exact-checked < independently-audited < certified`. *`exact-checked`* means an exact machine re-check exists but is same-author. *`independently-audited`* means a **separately authored, no-code-shared** auditor re-derived the result. Aggregation: the state layer is disjunctive (MAX over independent kills); cell/branch/subcase/target are conjunctive (MIN over required children, folded with the exhaustiveness-edge level, which must be $\ge$ `exact-checked` to close a parent above `claimed`).

**Trust tiers (inventory claims):** tier 1 = independently audited; tier 2 = exact checker wired into the suite; tier 2\* = exact checker exists but unwired; tier 3 = document-only or evidence-only; tier 4 = published result used as stated. A row may be compound, e.g. **2/4**, when a checker verifies the finite arithmetic but the claim also leans on a published proposition used as stated. `[QQ1]`/C3 and C2 are the only two 2/4 rows.
