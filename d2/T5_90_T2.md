## 1. E is constant

UFD parity in (1) gives

```text
E=gamma*s*u^2,
G=eta*s*w^2,
sigma=delta*s^2*u^3*w,
```

with `s` squarefree. Suppose a root of `s` exists. Since `deg E<=1`,
its order in `E` is one. Put `h=v_s(G)`; then `h` is odd and

```text
2 v_s(sigma)=3+h.
```

At this root the level-5 cascade equation is

```text
t^3 g6 = E^3 g5 + c^5 q^5 h5,
h5 = -9216 d2 sigma^2 + 2048 e^2.
```

The three relevant orders are

```text
v_s(E^3 g5) >= 3,
v_s(h5) = 2,
v_s(t^3 g6) = h.
```

The `e^2` term is the unique order-two term because
`v_s(sigma)>=2). Equality would force `h=2), contradicting that `h`
is odd. Thus `s` is constant. Consequently `E` is a square; since
`deg E<=1`, it is constant.

Equation (1) now makes `G` a square and gives `deg sigma<=6).

## 2. Infinity-place domination

Here `deg e=9`. For `f<=4), the generic graded bound is

```text
deg(Phi^f e^(21-3f) h_f) <= 229+3f <= 241.
```

For `f=5), the term `2048e^2` is the unique leading term of `h5):
the other term has degree at most `4+2*6=16<18). Hence

```text
deg(term_5) = 5*34 + 6*9 + 18 = 242.
```

For `f=6),

```text
deg(term_6) = 6*34 + 3*9 + 2 deg(sigma).
```

If `deg sigma<=5`, term 5 is the unique top-degree summand. If
`deg sigma=6`, term 6 has degree 243 and is the unique top-degree summand.
Either case contradicts the polynomial identity.

Therefore the geometrically `q`-coprime `a_t=9`, T2 branch is
infeasible. The source formulas and all degree cases are checked by
`t5_90t2_verify.py`.
